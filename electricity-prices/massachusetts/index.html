Electricity rates in Massachusetts

Homes in Massachusetts paid an average of 30.97¢ per kWh in September 2026 — well above the U.S. average of 18.17¢ and up 2.2% from a year earlier. It ranks #3 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Massachusetts compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: California (30.86¢) and Maine (31.29¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, Massachusetts's price works out to $1,344 a year more than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.3× the commercial rate of 23.75¢ (30th widest gap of 51).
  • The 2.2% change in a year is moderate; 42 of 51 states saw residential electricity prices rise.

How Massachusetts electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Massachusetts went from 19.69¢ in September 2016 to 30.97¢ in September 2026, up 57.3%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.2%, so Massachusetts outpaced the national change. Its price barely changes with the season, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Massachusetts household uses and pays

From October 2025 to September 2026, the average residential customer in Massachusetts used 609 kWh a month and paid $185 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 885 kWh and $160, which puts Massachusetts at #5 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 31% less power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in August at $236 (774 kWh) and were lowest in May at $137.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Massachusetts?

30.97¢ per kWh for residential customers in September 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is well above the national average of 18.17¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Massachusetts?

At the state average of 30.97¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $310. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Massachusetts?

$185 a month for the average residential customer from October 2025 to September 2026, for 609 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $160 for 885 kWh, so Massachusetts ranks #5 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in August ($236) and lowest in May ($137).

Are electricity prices in Massachusetts going up?

The September 2026 average was up 2.2% from the same month a year earlier (30.31¢), and up 57.3% from September 2016 (U.S.: up 41.2%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the New England region have cheaper electricity than Massachusetts?

In the New England region, Vermont (24.83¢), Connecticut (27.09¢) and New Hampshire (29.17¢) had lower average residential prices than Massachusetts (30.97¢) in September 2026, according to EIA.
