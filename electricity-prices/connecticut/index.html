Electricity rates in Connecticut

Homes in Connecticut paid an average of 27.09¢ per kWh in September 2026 — well above the U.S. average of 18.17¢ and down 10.9% from a year earlier. It ranks #9 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Connecticut compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Alaska (28.00¢) and District of Columbia (25.82¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, Connecticut's price works out to $937 a year more than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.3× the commercial rate of 20.34¢ (24th widest gap of 51).
  • Connecticut is one of the few states where the home price fell in a year (10.9% down); 42 of 51 saw increases.

How Connecticut electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Connecticut went from 18.98¢ in September 2016 to 27.09¢ in September 2026, up 42.7%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.2%, so Connecticut tracked the national trend. Its price runs 7% higher in winter than in summer, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Connecticut household uses and pays

From October 2025 to September 2026, the average residential customer in Connecticut used 705 kWh a month and paid $192 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 885 kWh and $160, which puts Connecticut at #3 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 20% less power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in January at $245 (867 kWh) and were lowest in October at $145.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Connecticut?

27.09¢ per kWh for residential customers in September 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is well above the national average of 18.17¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Connecticut?

At the state average of 27.09¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $271. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Connecticut?

$192 a month for the average residential customer from October 2025 to September 2026, for 705 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $160 for 885 kWh, so Connecticut ranks #3 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in January ($245) and lowest in October ($145).

Are electricity prices in Connecticut going up?

The September 2026 average was down 10.9% from the same month a year earlier (30.41¢), and up 42.7% from September 2016 (U.S.: up 41.2%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the New England region have cheaper electricity than Connecticut?

In the New England region, Vermont (24.83¢) had lower average residential prices than Connecticut (27.09¢) in September 2026, according to EIA.
