Electricity rates in Rhode Island

Homes in Rhode Island paid an average of 28.18¢ per kWh in August 2026 — well above the U.S. average of 18.16¢ and up 6.2% from a year earlier. It ranks #7 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Rhode Island compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: New York (28.65¢) and New Hampshire (27.64¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, Rhode Island's price works out to $1,052 a year more than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.2× the commercial rate of 23.31¢ (38th widest gap of 51).
  • The 6.2% change in a year is moderate; 44 of 51 states saw residential electricity prices rise.

How Rhode Island electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Rhode Island went from 18.56¢ in August 2016 to 28.18¢ in August 2026, up 51.8%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.0%, so Rhode Island outpaced the national change. Its price runs 11% higher in winter than in summer, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Rhode Island household uses and pays

From September 2025 to August 2026, the average residential customer in Rhode Island used 550 kWh a month and paid $162 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 875 kWh and $158, which puts Rhode Island at #18 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 37% less power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in July at $217 (766 kWh) and were lowest in September at $135.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Rhode Island?

28.18¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is well above the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Rhode Island?

At the state average of 28.18¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $282. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Rhode Island?

$162 a month for the average residential customer from September 2025 to August 2026, for 550 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $158 for 875 kWh, so Rhode Island ranks #18 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in July ($217) and lowest in September ($135).

Are electricity prices in Rhode Island going up?

The August 2026 average was up 6.2% from the same month a year earlier (26.53¢), and up 51.8% from August 2016 (U.S.: up 41.0%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the New England region have cheaper electricity than Rhode Island?

In the New England region, Vermont (23.84¢), Connecticut (24.51¢) and New Hampshire (27.64¢) had lower average residential prices than Rhode Island (28.18¢) in August 2026, according to EIA.
