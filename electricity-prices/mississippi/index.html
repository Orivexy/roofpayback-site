Electricity rates in Mississippi

Homes in Mississippi paid an average of 14.43¢ per kWh in August 2026 — well below the U.S. average of 18.16¢ and up 6.0% from a year earlier. It ranks #41 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Mississippi electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Mississippi went from 10.23¢ in August 2016 to 14.43¢ in August 2026, up 41.1%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.0%, so Mississippi tracked the national trend. Its price runs 2% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Mississippi household uses and pays

From September 2025 to August 2026, the average residential customer in Mississippi used 1,170 kWh a month and paid $174 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 875 kWh and $158, which puts Mississippi at #9 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 34% more power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in August at $239 (1,655 kWh) and were lowest in November at $132.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Mississippi?

14.43¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is well below the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Mississippi?

At the state average of 14.43¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $144. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Mississippi?

$174 a month for the average residential customer from September 2025 to August 2026, for 1,170 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $158 for 875 kWh, so Mississippi ranks #9 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in August ($239) and lowest in November ($132).

Are electricity prices in Mississippi going up?

The August 2026 average was up 6.0% from the same month a year earlier (13.61¢), and up 41.1% from August 2016 (U.S.: up 41.0%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the East South Central region have cheaper electricity than Mississippi?

In the East South Central region, Tennessee (13.42¢) and Kentucky (13.80¢) had lower average residential prices than Mississippi (14.43¢) in August 2026, according to EIA.
