Electricity rates in South Carolina

Homes in South Carolina paid an average of 15.76¢ per kWh in August 2026 — below the U.S. average of 18.16¢ and up 4.0% from a year earlier. It ranks #32 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How South Carolina compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Georgia (15.90¢) and Texas (15.91¢).

How South Carolina electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in South Carolina went from 12.98¢ in August 2016 to 15.76¢ in August 2026, up 21.4%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.0%, so South Carolina lagged the national change. Its price runs 3% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical South Carolina household uses and pays

From September 2025 to August 2026, the average residential customer in South Carolina used 1,070 kWh a month and paid $169 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 875 kWh and $158, which puts South Carolina at #12 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 22% more power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in July at $232 (1,499 kWh) and were lowest in November at $129.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in South Carolina?

15.76¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is below the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in South Carolina?

At the state average of 15.76¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $158. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in South Carolina?

$169 a month for the average residential customer from September 2025 to August 2026, for 1,070 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $158 for 875 kWh, so South Carolina ranks #12 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in July ($232) and lowest in November ($129).

Are electricity prices in South Carolina going up?

The August 2026 average was up 4.0% from the same month a year earlier (15.16¢), and up 21.4% from August 2016 (U.S.: up 41.0%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the South Atlantic region have cheaper electricity than South Carolina?

In the South Atlantic region, North Carolina (14.95¢) and Florida (14.99¢) had lower average residential prices than South Carolina (15.76¢) in August 2026, according to EIA.
