Electricity rates in Oregon

Homes in Oregon paid an average of 15.92¢ per kWh in August 2026 — below the U.S. average of 18.16¢ and up 0.4% from a year earlier. It ranks #29 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Oregon compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Texas (15.91¢) and Georgia (15.90¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, Oregon's price works out to $235 a year less than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households here pay 1.5× what businesses pay per kWh (10.72¢), one of the 10 widest home-vs-business gaps (7th of 51).
  • The 0.4% change in a year is moderate; 44 of 51 states saw residential electricity prices rise.

How Oregon electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Oregon went from 10.90¢ in August 2016 to 15.92¢ in August 2026, up 46.1%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.0%, so Oregon outpaced the national change. Its price runs 10% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Oregon household uses and pays

From September 2025 to August 2026, the average residential customer in Oregon used 843 kWh a month and paid $131 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 875 kWh and $158, which puts Oregon at #40 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills peaked in January at $167 (1,139 kWh) and were lowest in May at $110.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Our solar examples assume 10,500 kWh a year, against 10,120 kWh for the average Oregon customer. Check your own figures with the electricity cost calculator. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Oregon?

15.92¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is below the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Oregon?

At the state average of 15.92¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $159. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Oregon?

$131 a month for the average residential customer from September 2025 to August 2026, for 843 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $158 for 875 kWh, so Oregon ranks #40 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in January ($167) and lowest in May ($110).

Are electricity prices in Oregon going up?

The August 2026 average was up 0.4% from the same month a year earlier (15.85¢), and up 46.1% from August 2016 (U.S.: up 41.0%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the Pacific Contiguous region have cheaper electricity than Oregon?

In the Pacific Contiguous region, Washington (14.63¢) had lower average residential prices than Oregon (15.92¢) in August 2026, according to EIA.
