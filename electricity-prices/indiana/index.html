Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Indiana?

17.02¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is below the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Indiana?

At the state average of 17.02¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $170. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

Are electricity prices in Indiana going up?

The August 2026 average was up 5.0% from the same month a year earlier (16.21¢). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the East North Central region have cheaper electricity than Indiana?

None. Indiana (17.02¢) had the lowest average residential price in the East North Central region in August 2026, according to EIA.

What is a typical monthly electric bill in Indiana?

A home using 900 kWh a month would pay about $153 at the August 2026 state average of 17.02¢/kWh, versus $163 at the U.S. average. Actual bills vary with usage, season and each utility's fixed charges.

Is solar worth it in Indiana at these prices?

For our example 7 kW system on a 10,500 kWh/yr home with full net metering, payback in Indiana is 14.3 years at today's average price. It depends heavily on your rate, roof and how your utility credits exports — see the state solar page for details.
