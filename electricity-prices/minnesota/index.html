Electricity rates in Minnesota

Homes in Minnesota paid an average of 17.20¢ per kWh in August 2026 — below the U.S. average of 18.16¢ and up 1.8% from a year earlier. It ranks #21 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How Minnesota compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Colorado (17.17¢) and Iowa (17.06¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, Minnesota's price works out to $101 a year less than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.3× the commercial rate of 13.41¢ (25th widest gap of 51).
  • The 1.8% change in a year is moderate; 44 of 51 states saw residential electricity prices rise.

How Minnesota electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in Minnesota went from 13.07¢ in August 2016 to 17.20¢ in August 2026, up 31.6%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.0%, so Minnesota lagged the national change. Its price runs 16% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical Minnesota household uses and pays

From September 2025 to August 2026, the average residential customer in Minnesota used 745 kWh a month and paid $121 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 875 kWh and $158, which puts Minnesota at #44 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 15% less power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in July at $169 (971 kWh) and were lowest in November at $100.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in Minnesota?

17.20¢ per kWh for residential customers in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is below the national average of 18.16¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in Minnesota?

At the state average of 17.20¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $172. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in Minnesota?

$121 a month for the average residential customer from September 2025 to August 2026, for 745 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $158 for 875 kWh, so Minnesota ranks #44 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in July ($169) and lowest in November ($100).

Are electricity prices in Minnesota going up?

The August 2026 average was up 1.8% from the same month a year earlier (16.89¢), and up 31.6% from August 2016 (U.S.: up 41.0%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the West North Central region have cheaper electricity than Minnesota?

In the West North Central region, North Dakota (13.31¢), Nebraska (13.54¢) and South Dakota (15.15¢) had lower average residential prices than Minnesota (17.20¢) in August 2026, according to EIA.
