Electricity rates in South Dakota

Homes in South Dakota paid an average of 16.31¢ per kWh in September 2026 — below the U.S. average of 18.17¢ and up 11.1% from a year earlier. It ranks #27 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How South Dakota compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Georgia (16.27¢) and West Virginia (16.23¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, South Dakota's price works out to $195 a year less than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.3× the commercial rate of 12.14¢ (20th widest gap of 51).
  • The 11.1% rise in a year is steep: South Dakota is among the 42 states where residential electricity prices went up, and one of the fastest.

How South Dakota electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in South Dakota went from 12.54¢ in September 2016 to 16.31¢ in September 2026, up 30.1%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.2%, so South Dakota lagged the national change. Its price runs 18% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical South Dakota household uses and pays

From October 2025 to September 2026, the average residential customer in South Dakota used 988 kWh a month and paid $142 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 885 kWh and $160, which puts South Dakota at #33 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 12% more power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in July at $176 (1,146 kWh) and were lowest in October at $112.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in South Dakota?

16.31¢ per kWh for residential customers in September 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is below the national average of 18.17¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in South Dakota?

At the state average of 16.31¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $163. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in South Dakota?

$142 a month for the average residential customer from October 2025 to September 2026, for 988 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $160 for 885 kWh, so South Dakota ranks #33 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in July ($176) and lowest in October ($112).

Are electricity prices in South Dakota going up?

The September 2026 average was up 11.1% from the same month a year earlier (14.68¢), and up 30.1% from September 2016 (U.S.: up 41.2%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the West North Central region have cheaper electricity than South Dakota?

In the West North Central region, Nebraska (13.87¢), North Dakota (14.29¢) and Iowa (14.99¢) had lower average residential prices than South Dakota (16.31¢) in September 2026, according to EIA.
