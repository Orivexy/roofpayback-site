Electricity rates in California

Homes in California paid an average of 30.86¢ per kWh in September 2026 — well above the U.S. average of 18.17¢ and down 3.2% from a year earlier. It ranks #4 of 51 (50 states and D.C.), from most to least expensive.

How California compares

  • States with the most similar residential price: Massachusetts (30.97¢) and Maine (31.29¢).
  • For a home using 10,500 kWh a year, California's price works out to $1,332 a year more than the U.S. average on energy charges alone.
  • Households pay 1.0× the commercial rate of 30.38¢ (50th widest gap of 51).
  • California is one of the few states where the home price fell in a year (3.2% down); 42 of 51 saw increases.

How California electricity prices have changed since 2016

The average residential price in California went from 18.22¢ in September 2016 to 30.86¢ in September 2026, up 69.4%. Over the same decade the U.S. average moved up 41.2%, so California outpaced the national change. Its price runs 5% higher in summer than in winter, comparing June to August with December to February over the last three years.

What a typical California household uses and pays

From October 2025 to September 2026, the average residential customer in California used 507 kWh a month and paid $163 a month for electricity. The U.S. averages were 885 kWh and $160, which puts California at #19 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Homes there use 43% less power than the national average, so the bill gap is not only about price. Bills peaked in August at $235 (789 kWh) and were lowest in November at $133.

A residential customer is one utility account, so apartments and small homes pull these averages down; a large house with electric heat or a pool will use more. Common questions

What is the average electricity rate in California?

30.86¢ per kWh for residential customers in September 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. That is well above the national average of 18.17¢. Your own rate depends on your utility and plan.

How much does 1,000 kWh of electricity cost in California?

At the state average of 30.86¢/kWh, 1,000 kWh of energy costs about $309. That average already includes taxes and fees spread across all kWh sold; your own cost depends on your utility's rates and how much you use.

What is the average electric bill in California?

$163 a month for the average residential customer from October 2025 to September 2026, for 507 kWh a month, according to EIA-861M utility reports. The U.S. average was $160 for 885 kWh, so California ranks #19 of 51 for the average bill, from highest to lowest. Bills were highest in August ($235) and lowest in November ($133).

Are electricity prices in California going up?

The September 2026 average was down 3.2% from the same month a year earlier (31.89¢), and up 69.4% from September 2016 (U.S.: up 41.2%). Recent EIA months are preliminary and can be revised.

Which states in the Pacific Contiguous region have cheaper electricity than California?

In the Pacific Contiguous region, Washington (15.00¢) and Oregon (15.73¢) had lower average residential prices than California (30.86¢) in September 2026, according to EIA.
