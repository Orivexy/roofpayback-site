In Las Vegas, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,753 kWh a year, #3 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Nevada paid 12.72¢/kWh on average in August 2026 (#51 highest in the U.S.; national average 18.16¢), up 1.4% from a year earlier. Below-average power prices stretch the payback, so sizing and the export credit matter more. Nevada electricity rates in detail.

What stands out in Nevada

  • Nevada is sunnier than most states (1,753 kWh per kW, median 1,370) but its power is cheap (12.72¢/kWh vs. a 16.33¢ median), so each kWh saved is worth less and the example system pays back in about 16.1 years. Here the electricity price, not the sun, is what limits payback.
  • Among the 8 states of the Mountain division, Nevada has the lowest residential price; Colorado pays the most at 17.17¢.
  • Seasonal swing is typical: May is the best month (180 kWh per kW) and December the weakest (108), a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.5.
  • East- or west-facing panels make about 15% less than south-facing ones, close to the national typical loss.
  • The export credit decides the case: with full 1:1 net metering the example system pays back in about 16.1 years, but if exported power earned only a quarter of retail it would not pay back within 25 years. Check your utility's export rate before sizing.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 1.4% in a year (41st largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.3× the commercial rate (9.80¢).
  • Location within Nevada matters too: Henderson makes 1,734 kWh per kW a year and Reno 1,664, a 4% difference at the same state price.

How much do solar panels cost in Nevada?

Covering a year of use for a Nevada home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 6.0 kW system. At the national price range Berkeley Lab measured for home systems installed in 2024, that is roughly $18,000–$31,200 before incentives (midpoint $24,000). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Nevada.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2025 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Nevada: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Nevada?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Nevada is 16.1 years with full net metering and not within 25 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Nevada?

16.1 years in our example with full net metering, 20.5 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Nevada?

About 1,753 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Las Vegas (NLR PVWatts), from 108 kWh in Dec to 180 kWh in May.

What size solar system do I need in Nevada?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,753 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 6.0 kW of south-facing panels in Nevada. The average Nevada residential customer used 10,893 kWh from September 2025 to August 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 6.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Nevada?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 108 kWh in Dec versus 180 kWh in May, or 60% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Las Vegas). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
