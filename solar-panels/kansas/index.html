In Wichita, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,526 kWh a year, #10 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Kansas paid 15.47¢/kWh on average in August 2026 (#34 highest in the U.S.; national average 18.16¢), up 5.6% from a year earlier. Below-average power prices stretch the payback, so sizing and the export credit matter more. Kansas electricity rates in detail.

What stands out in Kansas

  • Kansas is sunnier than most states (1,526 kWh per kW, median 1,370) but its power is cheap (15.47¢/kWh vs. a 16.33¢ median), so each kWh saved is worth less and the example system pays back in about 13.8 years. Here the electricity price, not the sun, is what limits payback.
  • In the West North Central division Kansas ranks 4th of 7 on price, between Minnesota (17.20¢) at the top and North Dakota (13.31¢) at the bottom.
  • Seasonal swing is typical: July is the best month (157 kWh per kW) and December the weakest (86), a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.6.
  • East- or west-facing panels make about 15% less than south-facing ones, close to the national typical loss.
  • Export credit rules move the result by 9.2 years: 13.8 years with full net metering vs. 23.0 if exports earned a quarter of retail.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 5.6% in a year (25th largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.3× the commercial rate (12.24¢).

How much do solar panels cost in Kansas?

Covering a year of use for a Kansas home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 7.0 kW system. At the national price range Berkeley Lab measured for home systems installed in 2024, that is roughly $21,000–$36,400 before incentives (midpoint $28,000). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Kansas.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2025 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Kansas: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Kansas?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Kansas is 13.8 years with full net metering and 23.0 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Kansas?

13.8 years in our example with full net metering, 18.9 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Kansas?

About 1,526 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Wichita (NLR PVWatts), from 86 kWh in Dec to 157 kWh in Jul.

What size solar system do I need in Kansas?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,526 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 7.0 kW of south-facing panels in Kansas. The average Kansas residential customer used 10,632 kWh from September 2025 to August 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 7.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Kansas?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 86 kWh in Dec versus 157 kWh in Jul, or 55% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Wichita). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
