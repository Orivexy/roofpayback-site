Solar panels in Fayetteville, AR: payback and output

A kW of south-facing panels in Fayetteville produces about 1,383 kWh a year, 2% less than Little Rock, the Arkansas reference city. Summer months produce 1.5× what winter does. To cover a typical 10,500 kWh/yr home on paper you'd need roughly 7.6 kW.

How much do solar panels cost in Fayetteville?

A 7.5 kW system — about what a Fayetteville home using 10,500 kWh a year needs — costs roughly $22,500–$39,000 before incentives, with a midpoint of $30,000. That uses the national range Berkeley Lab measured for home systems installed in 2024 ($3.00–$5.20 per watt; median $4.00). Cash purchases had a median of $3.50/W and loan-financed systems $4.70/W, mostly because of dealer fees.

Incentives: the 30% federal residential credit no longer applies to systems placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). State, local and utility rebates differ — check DSIRE for programs that cover Fayetteville, then compare at least a few local quotes.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2025 Data Update (2024$/W, host-owned systems). Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Fayetteville: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Fayetteville, AR?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Fayetteville is 16.1 years with full net metering and 21.3 years if exports earn half the retail rate, at the Arkansas average price of 14.37¢/kWh (EIA, August 2026).

How much electricity do solar panels produce in Fayetteville?

About 1,383 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch (NLR PVWatts, typical-year weather), from 84 kWh in Nov to 143 kWh in Aug.

What size solar system does a home in Fayetteville need?

About 7.6 kW to cover 10,500 kWh a year. Divide your own annual use by 1,383 kWh per kW for a first estimate; if exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

How much do solar panels cost in Fayetteville?

Roughly $22,500–$39,000 before incentives for a 7.5 kW system, using the national range Berkeley Lab reports for home systems installed in 2024 ($3.00–$5.20 per watt). Get local quotes to see where Fayetteville prices fall.

Does roof direction matter in Fayetteville?

Yes. Per kW at a 20° pitch: south 1,383 kWh, southeast 1,325 kWh, southwest 1,333 kWh, east 1,194 kWh, west 1,204 kWh, east-west (split) 1,199 kWh a year. South-facing roofs produce the most; east or west roofs still work, with less output.
