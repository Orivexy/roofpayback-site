Solar panels in Missoula, MT: payback and output

A kW of south-facing panels in Missoula produces about 1,189 kWh a year, 12% less than Billings, the Montana reference city. Summer months produce 2.9× what winter does. To cover a typical 10,500 kWh/yr home on paper you'd need roughly 9.0 kW.

What stands out in Missoula

  • Missoula is among the least sunny cities we cover (94th of 98, 1,189 kWh per kW a year), so the electricity price matters more than usual for payback.
  • It produces 7% less than its latitude (46.9°N) alone would suggest, a sign of cloud, haze or coastal weather rather than distance from the equator.
  • The closest other city in our data is Spokane, WA (170 miles away): it makes 1,257 kWh per kW, 5% more than Missoula, but it pays 14.63¢/kWh vs. 15.57¢ here.
  • A 7 kW system here makes about 1,117 kWh in July and 270 kWh in December; at 15.57¢/kWh that is roughly $174 vs. $42 of electricity in those months.

Computed from NLR PVWatts output for every city we cover and the state's EIA price.
