How much do solar panels cost in Mississippi?

A 7.5 kW system — about what a Mississippi home using 10,500 kWh a year needs — costs roughly $22,500–$39,000 before incentives, with a midpoint of $30,000. That uses the national range Berkeley Lab measured for home systems installed in 2024 ($3.00–$5.20 per watt; median $4.00). Cash purchases had a median of $3.50/W and loan-financed systems $4.70/W, mostly because of dealer fees.

Incentives: the 30% federal residential credit no longer applies to systems placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). State, local and utility rebates differ — check DSIRE for programs that cover Mississippi, then compare at least a few local quotes.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2025 Data Update (2024$/W, host-owned systems). Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

What to check before going solar in Mississippi

  • How your utility credits exports. Net metering rules differ by state and by utility, and several states have moved to lower "net billing" credits worth a fraction of the retail rate. The example table shows three cases; look up your utility's current program in the DSIRE database.
  • Federal tax credit. The 30% residential clean energy credit does not apply to systems placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). Count only state or utility incentives that are still open.
  • Your real price per kWh. Divide your bill's energy and delivery charges by the kWh used; fixed monthly fees don't go away with solar.

Solar in Mississippi: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Mississippi?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Mississippi is 15.7 years with full net metering and not within 25 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Mississippi?

15.7 years in our example with full net metering, 21.0 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator above.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Mississippi?

About 1,414 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Jackson (NLR PVWatts), from 78 kWh in Dec to 141 kWh in May.

How much do solar panels cost in Mississippi?

About $22,500–$39,000 before incentives for a system sized to a 10,500 kWh/yr home, using Berkeley Lab's national range for home systems installed in 2024 ($3.00–$5.20 per watt, median $4.00). Local quotes decide where you fall in that range.

What size solar system do I need in Mississippi?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,414 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 7.5 kW of south-facing panels in Mississippi. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Mississippi?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 78 kWh in Dec versus 141 kWh in May, or 55% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Jackson). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.

What do homes in Mississippi pay for electricity?

14.43¢ per kWh on average in August 2026 (U.S. EIA), compared with 18.16¢ nationally.

Is there still a 30% federal tax credit for solar?

No. The IRS says the residential clean energy credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Check state and utility programs in the DSIRE database.
