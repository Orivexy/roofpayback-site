In Jackson, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,414 kWh a year, #20 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Mississippi paid 14.47¢/kWh on average in September 2026 (#43 highest in the U.S.; national average 18.17¢), up 3.7% from a year earlier. Below-average power prices stretch the payback, so sizing and the export credit matter more. Mississippi electricity rates in detail.

What stands out in Mississippi

  • Mississippi is sunnier than most states (1,414 kWh per kW, median 1,370) but its power is cheap (14.47¢/kWh vs. a 16.50¢ median), so each kWh saved is worth less and the example system pays back in about 15.7 years. Here the electricity price, not the sun, is what limits payback.
  • In the East South Central division Mississippi ranks 2nd of 4 on price, between Alabama (16.64¢) at the top and Tennessee (13.29¢) at the bottom.
  • Seasonal swing is typical: May is the best month (141 kWh per kW) and December the weakest (78), a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.5.
  • East- or west-facing panels lose only 12% versus south here (one of the 10 smallest penalties in the country), so a roof that doesn't face south is less of a problem in Mississippi than in most states.
  • Export credit rules move the result by 9.3 years: 15.7 years with full net metering vs. 25.0 if exports earned a quarter of retail.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 3.7% in a year (27th largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.1× the commercial rate (13.43¢).

How much do solar panels cost in Mississippi?

Covering a year of use for a Mississippi home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 7.5 kW system. At Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025, $3.00 per watt for cash purchases and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, that is roughly $22,500–$33,800 before incentives ($27,000 at the $3.60 median for all host-owned systems). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Mississippi.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2026 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Mississippi: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Mississippi?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Mississippi is 15.7 years with full net metering and 25.0 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How much do solar panels cost in Mississippi?

Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025 were $3.00 per watt for cash purchases, $3.60 for all host-owned systems and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, before incentives. A system sized to cover the 14,308 kWh the average Mississippi household used in the past year, about 10.0 kW here, would cost roughly $30,000 to $45,000 at those prices. The federal tax credit does not apply to systems installed after 2025; state and utility programs may still lower the cost.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Mississippi?

15.7 years in our example with full net metering, 20.9 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Mississippi?

About 1,414 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Jackson (NLR PVWatts), from 78 kWh in Dec to 141 kWh in May.

What size solar system do I need in Mississippi?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,414 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 7.5 kW of south-facing panels in Mississippi. The average Mississippi residential customer used 14,308 kWh from October 2025 to September 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 10.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Mississippi?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 78 kWh in Dec versus 141 kWh in May, or 55% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Jackson). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
