In Louisville, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,355 kWh a year, #30 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Kentucky paid 13.54¢/kWh on average in September 2026 (#47 highest in the U.S.; national average 18.17¢), up 1.0% from a year earlier. Below-average power prices stretch the payback, so sizing and the export credit matter more. Kentucky electricity rates in detail.

About the full-credit figure: Since January 1, 2020, new net-metering customers in Kentucky receive dollar credits for exports at a compensation rate the Public Service Commission sets in each utility's rate case, not automatically the retail rate, so check your utility's current rate before relying on the full-credit case. Kentucky Public Service Commission: net metering order (December 18, 2019).

What stands out in Kentucky

  • Kentucky sits below the median on both sunshine (1,355 kWh per kW) and price (13.54¢/kWh), the hardest case for rooftop solar; the example system pays back in about 17.3 years, so system cost and size matter more than usual.
  • In the East South Central division Kentucky ranks 3rd of 4 on price, between Alabama (16.64¢) at the top and Tennessee (13.29¢) at the bottom.
  • Seasonal swing is typical: June is the best month (143 kWh per kW) and December the weakest (73), a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.8.
  • East- or west-facing panels make about 14% less than south-facing ones, close to the national typical loss.
  • The export credit decides the case: with full 1:1 net metering the example system pays back in about 17.3 years, but if exported power earned only a quarter of retail it would not pay back within 25 years. Check your utility's export rate before sizing.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 1.0% in a year (38th largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.1× the commercial rate (12.12¢).

How much do solar panels cost in Kentucky?

Covering a year of use for a Kentucky home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 7.5 kW system. At Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025, $3.00 per watt for cash purchases and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, that is roughly $22,500–$33,800 before incentives ($27,000 at the $3.60 median for all host-owned systems). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Kentucky.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2026 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Kentucky: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Kentucky?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Kentucky is 17.3 years with full net metering and not within 25 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How much do solar panels cost in Kentucky?

Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025 were $3.00 per watt for cash purchases, $3.60 for all host-owned systems and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, before incentives. A system sized to cover the 13,509 kWh the average Kentucky household used in the past year, about 10.0 kW here, would cost roughly $30,000 to $45,000 at those prices. The federal tax credit does not apply to systems installed after 2025; state and utility programs may still lower the cost.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Kentucky?

17.3 years in our example with full net metering, 22.7 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Kentucky?

About 1,355 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Louisville (NLR PVWatts), from 73 kWh in Dec to 143 kWh in Jun.

What size solar system do I need in Kentucky?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,355 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 7.5 kW of south-facing panels in Kentucky. The average Kentucky residential customer used 13,509 kWh from October 2025 to September 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 10.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Kentucky?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 73 kWh in Dec versus 143 kWh in Jun, or 51% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Louisville). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
