Solar panels in Palm Springs, CA: payback and output

A kW of south-facing panels in Palm Springs produces about 1,762 kWh a year, 5% more than Los Angeles, the California reference city. Summer months produce 1.4× what winter does. To cover a typical 10,500 kWh/yr home on paper you'd need roughly 6.0 kW.

About the full-credit figure: Since April 15, 2023, new customers of California's big three utilities are on the Net Billing Tariff, which credits exports at a value tied to the grid rather than the full retail rate. The half- and quarter-credit cases are closer to what a new system earns. California Public Utilities Commission: Net Billing Tariff.

What stands out in Palm Springs

  • Palm Springs is one of the sunniest places we cover: 5th of 98 cities, at 1,762 kWh per kW a year.
  • It is 17% sunnier than its latitude (33.8°N) alone would suggest — clear skies, not just a southern location, drive the output here.
  • The closest other city in our data is Riverside, CA (49 miles away): it makes 1,696 kWh per kW, 4% less than Palm Springs.
  • A 7 kW system here makes about 1,252 kWh in May and 783 kWh in December; at 30.86¢/kWh that is roughly $386 vs. $242 of electricity in those months.
  • Of the 9 California cities we cover, Palm Springs ranks 1st for output (Palm Springs, Riverside, Bakersfield, San Diego, Fresno, San Jose, Sacramento, Oakland and San Francisco, sunniest first).
