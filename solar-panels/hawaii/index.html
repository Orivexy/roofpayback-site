In Honolulu, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,622 kWh a year, #5 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Hawaii paid 48.31¢/kWh on average in September 2026 (the highest in the U.S.; national average 18.17¢), up 22.2% from a year earlier. Above-average power prices make every solar kWh worth more here. Hawaii electricity rates in detail.

About the full-credit figure: Hawaii closed net energy metering to new customers in October 2015. New systems are sized for self-consumption and credited under newer programs, so the full-credit case overstates what exports earn. Hawaiian Electric: Net Energy Metering (closed program).

What stands out in Hawaii

  • Hawaii has both above-median sunshine (1,622 kWh per kW a year) and above-median power prices (48.31¢/kWh), the combination that makes rooftop solar pay fastest; the example system pays back in about 4.7 years.
  • Output is unusually steady through the year: summer only makes 1.3× winter (2nd steadiest of 51), from 114 kWh per kW in February to 152 in August, so monthly bill savings are more even than in most states.
  • East- or west-facing panels lose only 7% versus south here (one of the 10 smallest penalties in the country), so a roof that doesn't face south is less of a problem in Hawaii than in most states.
  • Export credit rules move the result by 3.4 years: 4.7 years with full net metering vs. 8.1 if exports earned a quarter of retail.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 22.2% in a year (1st largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.1× the commercial rate (45.06¢).

How much do solar panels cost in Hawaii?

Covering a year of use for a Hawaii home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 6.5 kW system. At Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025, $3.00 per watt for cash purchases and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, that is roughly $19,500–$29,300 before incentives ($23,400 at the $3.60 median for all host-owned systems). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Hawaii.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2026 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Hawaii: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Hawaii?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Hawaii is 4.7 years with full net metering and 8.1 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How much do solar panels cost in Hawaii?

Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025 were $3.00 per watt for cash purchases, $3.60 for all host-owned systems and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, before incentives. A system sized to cover the 6,181 kWh the average Hawaii household used in the past year, about 4.0 kW here, would cost roughly $12,000 to $18,000 at those prices. The federal tax credit does not apply to systems installed after 2025; state and utility programs may still lower the cost.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Hawaii?

4.7 years in our example with full net metering, 6.3 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Hawaii?

About 1,622 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Honolulu (NLR PVWatts), from 114 kWh in Feb to 152 kWh in Aug.

What size solar system do I need in Hawaii?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,622 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 6.5 kW of south-facing panels in Hawaii. The average Hawaii residential customer used 6,181 kWh from October 2025 to September 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 4.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Hawaii?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 114 kWh in Feb versus 152 kWh in Aug, or 75% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Honolulu). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
