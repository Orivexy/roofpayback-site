In Kansas City, each kW of south-facing panels produces about 1,433 kWh a year, #16 of 51 states for sunshine. Homes in Missouri paid 16.01¢/kWh on average in September 2026 (#30 highest in the U.S.; national average 18.17¢), up 2.5% from a year earlier. Below-average power prices stretch the payback, so sizing and the export credit matter more. Missouri electricity rates in detail.

What stands out in Missouri

  • Missouri is sunnier than most states (1,433 kWh per kW, median 1,370) but its power is cheap (16.01¢/kWh vs. a 16.50¢ median), so each kWh saved is worth less and the example system pays back in about 14.1 years. Here the electricity price, not the sun, is what limits payback.
  • In the West North Central division Missouri ranks 3rd of 7 on price, between Minnesota (17.37¢) at the top and Nebraska (13.87¢) at the bottom.
  • Seasonal swing is typical: July is the best month (150 kWh per kW) and December the weakest (81), a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.7.
  • East- or west-facing panels make about 15% less than south-facing ones, close to the national typical loss.
  • Export credit rules move the result by 8.7 years: 14.1 years with full net metering vs. 22.9 if exports earned a quarter of retail.
  • Residential electricity prices rose 2.5% in a year (30th largest increase of 51), and households pay 1.3× the commercial rate (12.42¢).
  • Location within Missouri matters too: Springfield makes 1,400 kWh per kW a year and St. Louis 1,371, a 2% difference at the same state price.

How much do solar panels cost in Missouri?

Covering a year of use for a Missouri home that uses 10,500 kWh takes a 7.5 kW system. At Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025, $3.00 per watt for cash purchases and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, that is roughly $22,500–$33,800 before incentives ($27,000 at the $3.60 median for all host-owned systems). What to expect from quotes, and why loans cost more, is in our solar cost guide.

Incentives: the federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so only state, local and utility programs remain. DSIRE lists the ones open in Missouri.

Source: Berkeley Lab, U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage: 2026 Data Update. Local prices vary with installer, roof and equipment.

Solar in Missouri: common questions

Are solar panels worth it in Missouri?

For an example 7 kW system costing $24,500 on a home using 10,500 kWh a year, payback in Missouri is 14.1 years with full net metering and 22.9 years if exports earn a quarter of the retail rate. Panels typically last decades, so a shorter payback leaves more years of savings.

How much do solar panels cost in Missouri?

Berkeley Lab's national medians for home systems installed in 2025 were $3.00 per watt for cash purchases, $3.60 for all host-owned systems and $4.50 for loan-financed ones, before incentives. A system sized to cover the 12,738 kWh the average Missouri household used in the past year, about 9.0 kW here, would cost roughly $27,000 to $40,500 at those prices. The federal tax credit does not apply to systems installed after 2025; state and utility programs may still lower the cost.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves in Missouri?

14.1 years in our example with full net metering, 19.0 years at half-retail export credits. Your quote, roof and utility rules can move this a lot — run your own numbers in the calculator.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Missouri?

About 1,433 kWh per year for each kW of south-facing panels at a 20° pitch in Kansas City (NLR PVWatts), from 81 kWh in Dec to 150 kWh in Jul.

What size solar system do I need in Missouri?

To cover a full year of use, divide your annual kWh by about 1,433 kWh per kW. A home using 10,500 kWh a year would need roughly 7.5 kW of south-facing panels in Missouri. The average Missouri residential customer used 12,738 kWh from October 2025 to September 2026 (EIA-861M), which works out to about 9.0 kW. If exports earn less than retail, a smaller system often pays back faster.

Do solar panels work in winter in Missouri?

Yes, but output drops: each kW makes about 81 kWh in Dec versus 150 kWh in Jul, or 54% of the best month (NLR PVWatts, Kansas City). With net metering, summer credits can offset part of winter bills.
