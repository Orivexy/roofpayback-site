How to read the result

  • First-year savings: what you stop paying for power you use directly, plus bill credits for power you export.
  • Payback: years until cumulative savings (minus maintenance) equal the installed cost minus incentives.
  • kWh that earn no credit: exports beyond a year of grid purchases. Most utilities zero out or pay very little for excess credits at the annual true-up, so oversizing rarely pays.

The model, data sources and limits are explained in the methodology. Find your state's numbers on the state pages.

What to have at hand

  • Your yearly use in kWh: add up the last twelve bills (or download the year from your utility account). One month is not enough, because use swings with the seasons.
  • Your price per kWh: the bill's energy charges divided by the kWh billed. The state average pre-filled here includes taxes and fees spread over every kWh, so your own figure is better.
  • How your utility credits exports: full retail credit, a lower export rate, or nothing. Ask the utility or check the state regulator; it moves payback more than anything else below.
  • Your quote: system size in kW and the installed price before incentives, plus any state or utility incentive you are sure to receive.

What the calculator leaves out

  • Time-of-use rates, demand charges and monthly minimum bills: it uses one price per kWh.
  • Shading, roof condition and panel placement: production comes from PVWatts for an unshaded roof at the direction and pitch you choose.
  • Loan interest and tax effects: payback is on the cash cost after the incentives you enter.
  • Home batteries: use the battery payback calculator.
