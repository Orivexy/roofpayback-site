Changelog

Last updated: 2026-11-20

What changed on RoofPayback and when. Corrections to a specific number are listed here and noted on the page they affect. Report an error through the contact page.

November 20, 2026

  • Every state price page now charts the residential price month by month since 2016 against the U.S. average, with annual averages, and shows what the average household uses and pays each month, from the EIA-861M survey of utility sales, revenue and customers. The figures match the EIA Electric Power Monthly prices already on the site.
  • The statistics table and its CSV add each state's average monthly bill and use; the average electric bill guide now compares real average bills with the 900 kWh example.
  • Year-on-year changes on state pages use one decimal everywhere, and a few typing errors were fixed.

November 19, 2026

  • California, Hawaii and Texas pages now explain that new customers there can't count on full retail net metering, with links to the state regulators; the full-credit payback is labeled accordingly.
  • City pages stay available but are no longer offered to search engines while most of their text is shared with their state page.
  • Privacy and cookie policies corrected: the site is hosted on GitHub Pages, Google's ad-cookie disclosure and opt-out links added, and contact is by email.
  • The newest guide is now shown in a bar at the top of every page.

November 18, 2026

  • Merged overlapping articles so each question has one page: the July 2026 price report and "price increases by state" now live in the August 2026 price report; "electricity prices and solar payback" in best states for solar payback; "incentives after the federal tax credit" in solar without the tax credit; "cost per watt" in solar panel cost. Old addresses forward to the new pages.
  • Corrected wording on articles that had been reviewed: unsupported generalizations removed, the Hawaii article now cites EIA on why prices there are high, and the Texas article links our Fort Worth, El Paso and Lubbock pages instead of saying we had no figures for them.
  • State, city and price pages: removed repeated blocks; each state page now compares the cities we cover in it. Added this changelog and the editorial policy.
  • New site structure: the homepage starts from the three questions visitors bring (what power costs, whether solar pays off, what to check before buying), the menu is ordered the same way, and the price and solar hubs link to their calculator and key guides.

November 15, 2026

  • All articles now use the same data: EIA August 2026 residential prices and the example home at $3,500 per kW ($24,500 for 7 kW). Before, some used July prices or $3,000 per kW, so the same state could show different paybacks in different articles.
  • Articles rewritten in distinct formats (data report, Q&A, guide, comparison, explainer and others) without changing their figures; each was compared against its previous version and fact-checked.
  • Every article has its own photo, sourced from Wikimedia Commons with a verified free license and credited under the image.

October 23, 2026

  • Electricity prices updated to EIA August 2026 (preliminary).

October 12, 2026

  • Electricity prices updated to EIA July 2026 (preliminary).

October 11, 2026

  • Site launched with PVWatts output for every state and the payback model described in the methodology.
