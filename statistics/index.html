Electricity and solar statistics by state

The key numbers on what U.S. homes pay for power and how rooftop solar pays off, for all 50 states and D.C. Every figure comes from official U.S. government data and is free to use with a link back.

Key statistics

  1. 01

    U.S. homes paid an average of 18.17¢ per kWh for electricity in September 2026, up 0.7% from a year earlier.

  2. 02

    Hawaii had the most expensive residential electricity at 48.31¢/kWh, 2.7× the national average.

  3. 03

    Nevada had the cheapest residential electricity at 13.25¢/kWh — 73% less than Hawaii.

  4. 04

    19 of 51 states (including D.C.) paid more than the U.S. average residential price.

  5. 05

    Residential prices rose year over year in 42 of 51 states; the biggest increase was in Hawaii (+22.2%).

  6. 06

    At state average prices, 900 kWh of electricity costs from $119 in Nevada to $435 in Hawaii.

  7. 07

    Homes paid 18.17¢/kWh on average, versus 14.27¢ for businesses and 9.52¢ for industry.

  8. 08

    Each kW of south-facing rooftop solar produces about 1,779 kWh a year in New Mexico, the sunniest state in our data, versus 922 kWh in Alaska.

  9. 09

    For an example home (7 kW system at $3,500/kW, 10,500 kWh/yr home, full net metering with annual true-up, no incentives), solar pays back fastest in Hawaii (4.7 years) and pays back within 10 years in 9 of 51 states.

Sources and method

  • Solar output: NLR PVWatts v8, typical-year weather for each state's reference city, south-facing at 20°.
  • Payback: year-by-year cash flow with panel aging and maintenance — full methodology.
  • Updated monthly when the EIA publishes a new month. The September 2026 figures were retrieved on November 24, 2026; we last checked the EIA for a newer month on November 28, 2026.
