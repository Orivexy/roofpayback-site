22:Tf02,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">At a glance</p>
<p>This guide covers ten states: Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia and West Virginia, which the Census Bureau places in the South Atlantic division, and Alabama, Kentucky, Mississippi and Tennessee, its East South Central division. (Delaware, Maryland and D.C., also South Atlantic, are covered with the <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-the-mid-atlantic/">Mid-Atlantic</a>; Louisiana with the <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-the-south-central-states/">South Central states</a>.)</p>
<p>Power is cheap here: 16.12¢ per kWh in the South Atlantic and 14.42¢ in the East South Central division in September 2026, against 18.17¢ nationally. With full export credit, our example system pays back in 12.9 years in Virginia and 17.8 in Tennessee. Homes are large users, so right-sized systems are big, and in several states the credit for exported power is set utility by utility.</p>

<h2 id="three-comparisons-inside-the-region">Three comparisons inside the region</h2>
<p><strong>The Carolinas.</strong> Columbia and Charlotte make exactly the same 1,446 kWh per kW. South Carolina&#39;s price is 16.5¢ and North Carolina&#39;s 15.41¢, and that alone puts South Carolina 0.9 years ahead.</p>
<p><strong>Florida.</strong> Orlando has the most sun in the region, but Florida&#39;s price fell 6.5% over the year to 14.74¢, and it now pays back later than Alabama and Georgia. Its output is also the most even through the year, with a summer-to-winter ratio of 1.2, and its best month is May rather than July.</p>
<p><strong>West Virginia.</strong> Charleston&#39;s output, 1,232 kWh per kW, is among the lowest in the country and lower than Boston&#39;s, yet its price is mid-range for the region. The result, 16 years, is slower than any South Atlantic neighbor.</p>

<h2 id="how-exports-are-credited">How exports are credited</h2>
<p>Kentucky is the state where we could confirm the rule from the regulator. Under the net metering law that took effect on January 1, 2020, the Kentucky Public Service Commission says &quot;net metered customers will receive dollar credits at a compensation rate set by the PSC in rate proceedings for each utility.&quot; The value depends on your utility&#39;s latest rate case, so ask for the current rate; the half- and quarter-credit columns show how much it matters (17.3 years at full credit, 22.7 at half).</p>
<p>For the other nine states, we could not confirm the current residential rules from the regulators&#39; own pages for this update. Ask your utility what an exported kWh is credited at and when credits are settled, and check its tariff with your state regulator, such as the Florida or Georgia Public Service Commission, the North Carolina Utilities Commission or the Virginia State Corporation Commission. <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">Net metering vs. net billing</a> explains how each answer changes payback. Where credits are low, a smaller system and more daytime use help; the <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-the-south-central-states/">South Central guide</a> shows by how much.</p>
<h2 id="price-trends">Price trends</h2>
<p>Comparing 12-month averages ending September 2017 and September 2026 in EIA&#39;s utility data, prices rose about 4.3% a year in Virginia, 3.5% in Mississippi, 3.4% in North Carolina and West Virginia, 3.3% in Florida, 3.1% in Alabama, 2.9% in Georgia and Kentucky, 2.7% in Tennessee and 2.3% in South Carolina, before inflation. Our model assumes 2.5%; <a href="/guides/solar-payback-if-electricity-prices-rise/">solar payback if electricity prices rise</a> shows what other paths do.</p>
<h2 id="before-you-get-quotes">Before you get quotes</h2>
