33:Te1b,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">Short version</p>
<p>Before you sign a solar quote, work out three numbers yourself: the price per watt, the production per kW the quote claims, and a simple payback from the quote&#39;s own figures. Then confirm the export credit the savings assume and read the contract terms that quotes tend to leave vague: warranties, who handles permits and interconnection, and how you can cancel.</p>
<p>The worked example below shows how those three numbers can expose a payback claim that rests on assumptions the installer never stated.</p>
</div><h2 id="the-three-numbers">The three numbers</h2>
<p><strong>1. Price per watt.</strong> Divide the total installed price by the system size in watts. Berkeley Lab&#39;s 2026 Data Update reports a median of $3.60 per watt for host-owned home systems installed in 2025, $3.00 for cash purchases and $4.50 for loan-financed systems; among the 100 largest installers, medians ranged from $2.40 to $6.30. Where a quote falls in that range matters less than whether it pays back where you live: <a href="/guides/solar-panel-cost-by-state/">solar panel cost per watt</a> gives the most you can pay in each state for a 10- or 15-year payback.</p>
<p><strong>2. Production per kW.</strong> Divide the quote&#39;s first-year kWh estimate by the system&#39;s kW. Then enter your address, roof direction and tilt in PVWatts, the free NLR tool our <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> uses, and compare. A claim well above the PVWatts figure for your roof needs an explanation: different panels, a different tilt, or simply optimism.</p>
<p><strong>3. Simple payback.</strong> Divide the net price by the first-year savings the quote projects, minus any yearly upkeep. That ignores rising electricity prices, which shorten payback, and slowly declining output, which lengthens it, so a fair quote&#39;s payback is usually somewhat shorter than simple payback, not half of it. <a href="/guides/solar-payback-period-explained/">Solar payback period explained</a> shows how far apart the two usually are.</p>
<h2 id="a-worked-example">A worked example</h2>
<p>Here is a hypothetical quote, made up for illustration, for a home near Atlanta that uses 13,159 kWh a year, the Georgia average from EIA-861M for October 2025 to September 2026:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>8 kW system, south-facing, $25,600 installed. Estimated production 12,800 kWh in year one. Estimated payback 9 years.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Working through the three numbers with our model (Georgia&#39;s September 2026 average price of 16.27¢ per kWh, full retail credit for exports, $100 a year of upkeep):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Price per watt:</strong> $25,600 ÷ 8,000 W = $3.20. Between the cash and overall medians, and below Georgia&#39;s 15-year limit of $3.73.</li>
<li><strong>Production per kW:</strong> 12,800 ÷ 8 = 1,600 kWh per kW. PVWatts puts a south-facing roof at a 20° pitch in Atlanta at about 1,402, so this 8 kW system would make about 11,210 kWh. The quote is 14% higher.</li>
<li><strong>Payback:</strong> with PVWatts production, our model gives 13.1 years. Even with the quote&#39;s own production figure, it gives 11.6 years.</li>
</ul>
<p>To reach 9 years, the quote would need electricity prices to rise about 9% a year with its own production figure, or about 12% a year with PVWatts production. Our model uses 2.5%. A buyer who asks &quot;what price growth did you assume?&quot; gets to the point quickly.</p>
<p>None of this makes the quote bad. At $3.20 a watt and 13 years, it may be a fair offer. It just is not a 9-year offer.</p>

45:Tbea,<h2 id="questions-about-the-savings-estimate">Questions about the savings estimate</h2>
<ul>
<li>What price per kWh did you use, and does it match my bill?</li>
<li>How much do you assume my electricity price will rise each year?</li>
<li>What export credit did you assume, and is it my utility&#39;s current rule? <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">Net metering vs. net billing</a> shows what different credits do to payback.</li>
<li>What share of the output do you expect my home to use directly?</li>
<li>Did you include panel degradation and any yearly upkeep?</li>
<li>Did you count any incentive? The IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit &quot;is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025,&quot; so a 2026 quote should not subtract it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="questions-about-the-contract">Questions about the contract</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Warranties:</strong> how long are the panel, inverter and workmanship warranties, and who honors the workmanship warranty if the installer goes out of business?</li>
<li><strong>Permits and interconnection:</strong> who applies for building permits and for connection to the grid, who pays the fees, and what happens to the contract if the utility delays or rejects the application?</li>
<li><strong>Roof:</strong> who is responsible for leaks at the mounting points, and for how long? If the roof is near the end of its life, should it be replaced first?</li>
<li><strong>Timeline:</strong> when does installation start, and what are the payment milestones?</li>
<li><strong>Cancellation:</strong> the FTC&#39;s Cooling-Off Rule gives you until midnight of the third business day to cancel certain sales made at your home, and says the seller &quot;has to tell you at the time of the sale about your right to cancel&quot; and give you two copies of a cancellation form. Ask whether the rule, or a longer cancellation period in the contract, applies to your purchase.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="red-flags">Red flags</h2>
<ul>
<li>A payback figure with no stated electricity price, growth rate or export credit behind it.</li>
<li>A production estimate with no tool named, or one far above PVWatts for your roof.</li>
<li>A total price with no per-watt figure, or only a monthly payment.</li>
<li>A different price for cash and loan that nobody explains; the <a href="/guides/solar-loan-vs-cash/">loan vs. cash guide</a> shows what that gap costs.</li>
<li>A discount that expires at the end of the visit.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="comparing-quotes-side-by-side">Comparing quotes side by side</h2>
<p>For each quote, write down the price per watt, the claimed production per kW, the export credit assumed, the warranty terms and the payback claimed. Then put the same system through the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> with your own bill. The U.S. Department of Energy&#39;s homeowner guide also suggests reading reviews of solar companies before you choose one. The quote worth signing is the one whose numbers you could reproduce yourself.</p>
