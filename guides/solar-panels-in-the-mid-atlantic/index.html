Solar Panels in the Mid-Atlantic: Payback by State

New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland and D.C. compared: solar payback, typical home use and nine-year price trends, with September 2026 data.

Published November 10, 2026·Updated November 28, 2026·4 min read·RoofPayback editorial·Figures checked against EIA and PVWatts data

The answer

Yes, rooftop solar pays back reasonably fast across the Mid-Atlantic, because power is expensive. In our example home, full-credit payback runs from 9.1 years in New York City and 9.2 in Washington, D.C., to 12.7 years in Wilmington, Delaware.

The six places differ less in sunshine than in two other ways: how much a typical home uses, from 591 kWh a month in New York to 960 in Maryland, and how fast prices have risen, from about 3.3% a year in Delaware to 7.6% in D.C. over the last nine years.

Which places does this guide cover?

New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland and the District of Columbia. The Census Bureau's Middle Atlantic division includes only the first three; it places Delaware, Maryland and D.C. in the South Atlantic division with Virginia and the Southeast. We group the six because their prices and solar results sit close together. Virginia is covered in solar panels in the Southeast, and New York has its own guide, is solar worth it in New York.

How long does payback take in each place?

The example home is a 7 kW system at $24,500 using 10,500 kWh a year, with no incentives (methodology). Prices are EIA's September 2026 residential averages; the Middle Atlantic division averaged 25.23¢, up 5.9% from a year earlier, against 18.17¢ nationally.

Place (reference city) Price (change from a year earlier) Output per kW Full credit Half credit Quarter credit
New York (New York City) 28.42¢ (+4.2%) 1,291 kWh 9.1 years 12.1 years 14.4 years
District of Columbia 25.82¢ (+9.2%) 1,411 kWh 9.2 years 12.5 years 15.2 years
New Jersey (Newark) 24.64¢ (+5.7%) 1,306 kWh 10.3 years 13.7 years 16.3 years
Pennsylvania (Philadelphia) 22.2¢ (+8.7%) 1,359 kWh 11 years 14.7 years 17.6 years
Maryland (Baltimore) 21.54¢ (+2.1%) 1,392 kWh 11 years 14.9 years 17.9 years
Delaware (Wilmington) 18.89¢ (+4.1%) 1,363 kWh 12.7 years 16.9 years 20.2 years

Even the slowest case, Delaware at quarter credit, pays back inside our 25-year horizon. D.C. makes the most power per kW in the group, but New York's higher price gives it a slightly faster payback.

How much do typical homes use here?

Far more in the south of the region than in the north. EIA-861M data for October 2025 to September 2026 give these averages per residential account, with each system sized to that use:

Place Average use per month Average bill per month Right-sized system Payback, full credit
New York 591 kWh $169 5 kW 9.2 years
District of Columbia 643 kWh $158 5 kW 9.3 years
New Jersey 677 kWh $161 6 kW 10.4 years
Pennsylvania 844 kWh $177 7 kW 11 years
Delaware 942 kWh $170 8 kW 12.6 years
Maryland 960 kWh $204 8 kW 11 years

Maryland's average bill, $204 a month, is the second highest in the country in this data, even though its price is mid-range for the region; it comes from use, not rate. A Maryland home needs a system about 60% larger than a New York one, and pays about 60% more for it, yet the paybacks differ by less than two years. An account can be an apartment or a second home, so treat these as averages, not targets. How many solar panels do I need shows how to size from your own bills.

Are prices still rising?

Over the year to September 2026, D.C. rose 9.2% and Pennsylvania 8.7%, while Maryland rose 2.1%. Over a longer span, comparing 12-month averages ending September 2017 and September 2026 in EIA's utility data, yearly growth was about 7.6% in D.C., 5.3% in New York, 4.7% in New Jersey, 4.6% in Maryland, 4.5% in Pennsylvania and 3.3% in Delaware, before inflation. Our example assumes 2.5% a year; solar payback if electricity prices rise shows what a faster or slower path does.

If you buy your supply from a competitive supplier, the rate your panels offset may differ from the utility's; how electricity choice works explains what to compare.

How much do export credits matter?

Going from full to quarter credit adds 5.3 years in New York and 7.5 in Delaware: the lower the price, the more the credit rate weighs. We could not confirm each state's current residential rules from the regulators' own pages for this update, so check yours directly with your utility and its regulator: the New York Department of Public Service, the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities, the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, the Delaware or Maryland Public Service Commission, or the D.C. Public Service Commission. Ask the utility three things: what an exported kWh is credited at, whether credits carry from month to month, and what happens to leftover credit at the yearly settlement. Net metering vs. net billing shows how each answer changes payback.

How do I check my own roof?

  1. Add up 12 months of kWh from your bills.
  2. Find out how your utility credits exports and which rate you buy power at, including any competitive supply contract.
  3. Compare quotes on price per watt; at $3.00 instead of $3.50, New York's example payback drops from 9.1 to 7.9 years.
  4. Run your numbers in the solar calculator and check PVWatts against any production claim.

State detail is on the New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Maryland pages.
