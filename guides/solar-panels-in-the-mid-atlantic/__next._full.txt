33:Tb8c,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">The answer</p>
<p>Yes, rooftop solar pays back reasonably fast across the Mid-Atlantic, because power is expensive. In our example home, full-credit payback runs from 9.1 years in New York City and 9.2 in Washington, D.C., to 12.7 years in Wilmington, Delaware.</p>

<h2 id="are-prices-still-rising">Are prices still rising?</h2>
<p>Over the year to September 2026, D.C. rose 9.2% and Pennsylvania 8.7%, while Maryland rose 2.1%. Over a longer span, comparing 12-month averages ending September 2017 and September 2026 in EIA&#39;s utility data, yearly growth was about 7.6% in D.C., 5.3% in New York, 4.7% in New Jersey, 4.6% in Maryland, 4.5% in Pennsylvania and 3.3% in Delaware, before inflation. Our example assumes 2.5% a year; <a href="/guides/solar-payback-if-electricity-prices-rise/">solar payback if electricity prices rise</a> shows what a faster or slower path does.</p>
<p>If you buy your supply from a competitive supplier, the rate your panels offset may differ from the utility&#39;s; <a href="/guides/electricity-choice-states-explained/">how electricity choice works</a> explains what to compare.</p>
<h2 id="how-much-do-export-credits-matter">How much do export credits matter?</h2>
<p>Going from full to quarter credit adds 5.3 years in New York and 7.5 in Delaware: the lower the price, the more the credit rate weighs. We could not confirm each state&#39;s current residential rules from the regulators&#39; own pages for this update, so check yours directly with your utility and its regulator: the New York Department of Public Service, the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities, the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, the Delaware or Maryland Public Service Commission, or the D.C. Public Service Commission. Ask the utility three things: what an exported kWh is credited at, whether credits carry from month to month, and what happens to leftover credit at the yearly settlement. <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">Net metering vs. net billing</a> shows how each answer changes payback.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-i-check-my-own-roof">How do I check my own roof?</h2>
<ol>
<li>Add up 12 months of kWh from your bills.</li>
<li>Find out how your utility credits exports and which rate you buy power at, including any competitive supply contract.</li>
<li>Compare quotes on price per watt; at $3.00 instead of $3.50, New York&#39;s example payback drops from 9.1 to 7.9 years.</li>
<li>Run your numbers in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> and check <a href="https://pvwatts.nlr.gov/">PVWatts</a> against any production claim.</li>
</ol>
