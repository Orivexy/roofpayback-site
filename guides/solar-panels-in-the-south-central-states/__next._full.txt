33:Tdd5,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">The answer up front</p>
<p>Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas and Louisiana, the Census Bureau&#39;s West South Central division, pair decent sun with some of the cheapest residential power in the country: 15.4¢ per kWh in September 2026, against 18.17¢ nationally. With exports worth a quarter of retail, our working assumption for this region, our example system takes 23.1 years in Texas, 23.3 in Oklahoma and 24.9 in Arkansas, and does not pay back within 25 years in Louisiana. Full credit for every exported kWh, an upper bound, would bring that down to 14 to 16.2 years.</p>
<p>The quarter case comes from the two rules we could check. In Louisiana, the Public Service Commission&#39;s net metering rules credit exports &quot;at Avoided Cost&quot; for customers who install after December 31, 2019, and its 2026 rates are 3.58¢ to 3.99¢ per kWh by utility, close to a quarter of the state&#39;s average price; ask your utility whether those rules cover your address. Texas has no statewide net metering, and the buyback depends on your retail plan. We did not verify the rules in Oklahoma or Arkansas. For a household facing a credit like that, the useful question is not whether solar pays back but which system does: in our model, a smaller array used mostly in daylight is the difference between no payback and about 17 years.</p>

<h2 id="big-homes-big-bills-big-systems">Big homes, big bills, big systems</h2>

42:Te55,<h2 id="when-exports-earn-far-less-than-retail">When exports earn far less than retail</h2>
<p>The quarter- and half-credit columns are where this region&#39;s results diverge from the high-price states. Louisiana&#39;s avoided-cost credit is updated each year for each utility; our quarter-credit case is a stand-in for it, slightly below the 2026 rates, not the actual rate. Texas buyers should read <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-in-texas/">is solar worth it in Texas</a>, which covers how credits depend on the retail plan there. In Oklahoma and Arkansas, ask your utility and check its tariff with the Oklahoma Corporation Commission or the Arkansas Public Service Commission.</p>

<p>A 4 kW array ($14,000) sends much less to the grid than a 7 kW one, so more of its output replaces power bought at the full retail price. Raising daytime use from 40% to 60% pushes that further. In Louisiana, the combination turns a system that never pays back within 25 years into one that pays back in 17. <a href="/guides/solar-daytime-electricity-use/">Solar and daytime electricity use</a> explains how to shift loads, and <a href="/guides/how-many-solar-panels-do-i-need/">how many solar panels do I need</a> shows why smaller sizes win when exports are worth little.</p>
<p>The installed price is the other lever. For a home sized to its full use at quarter credit, $3.00 per watt instead of $3.50 shortens payback by about 2.6 to 2.8 years in Texas, Oklahoma and Arkansas.</p>
<h2 id="prices-over-the-years">Prices over the years</h2>
<p>Our model assumes 2.5% yearly increases. Comparing 12-month averages ending September 2017 and September 2026 in EIA&#39;s utility data, residential prices grew about 4.3% a year in Texas, 3.5% in Louisiana, 3.3% in Arkansas and 2.9% in Oklahoma, before inflation, all above that assumption. A one-year jump like Louisiana&#39;s is not a forecast; <a href="/guides/solar-payback-if-electricity-prices-rise/">solar payback if electricity prices rise</a> shows the range.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-household-should-ask-before-signing">What the household should ask before signing</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>How are exports credited?</strong> Ask for the current rate per kWh and how often credits settle.</li>
<li><strong>How much do we use in daylight?</strong> Smart-meter data shows it; a home that is empty all day exports more.</li>
<li><strong>What size does the installer propose, and why?</strong> Where exports earn little, ask for a quote sized to daytime use as well as one sized to the whole year.</li>
<li><strong>What is the price per watt?</strong> Compare it with the $3.50 used here; <a href="/guides/solar-panel-cost-by-state/">solar panel cost by state</a> shows the effect.</li>
</ul>
<p>Then run your own rate, credit and quote through the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>. Louisiana also borders the <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-the-southeast/">Southeast</a>, where prices are similar.</p>
