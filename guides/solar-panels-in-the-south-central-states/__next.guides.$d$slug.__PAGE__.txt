23:Ta6e,<div class="short-answer"><h2 id="short-answer">Short answer</h2>
<p>In the four South Central states of Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas and Louisiana, rooftop solar has a mixed payback. Sunshine is good, but electricity is cheap. In our example home, simple payback runs from 14.3 years in Texas and Oklahoma to 16.7 years in Louisiana, and only if the utility credits every exported kilowatt-hour at the full retail price. Under weaker credit rules, payback stretches past 20 years in most of the region.</p>
<p>The example home is a 7 kW system costing $24,500 with no incentives and 10,500 kWh a year of use. See the <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> for the rest of the assumptions.</p>
</div><h2 id="why-this-region-is-a-tough-case-for-solar">Why this region is a tough case for solar</h2>
<p>Solar saves you the price of every kilowatt-hour (kWh, the unit your utility bills you for) that your panels replace. A low price per kWh means a smaller saving per panel, no matter how sunny the roof.</p>
<p>EIA data for August 2026 puts the West South Central division, which covers these four states, at 15.3¢ per kWh for homes. That is well under the U.S. average of 18.16¢. Businesses in the region paid just 9.54¢ that month.</p>
<p>Cheap power is good for your monthly bill. It is not good for solar math. A system that would pay for itself in under ten years in a high-price state needs far longer here.</p>

<p>In Arkansas and Louisiana, a quarter-rate credit pushes payback beyond our 25-year horizon. That does not mean solar is a bad idea there. It means the answer depends heavily on your utility&#39;s export policy, which you can check with the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> once you know your own rate.</p>
<p>For a deeper look at how credit rules work, read our guide on <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">net metering vs. net billing payback</a>.</p>
<h2 id="prices-are-moving-and-not-evenly">Prices are moving, and not evenly</h2>
<p>EIA also tracks how prices changed from August 2025 to August 2026.</p>
<ul>
<li>Arkansas: up 7.8%, from 13.33¢ to 14.37¢.</li>
<li>Texas: up 3.3%, from 15.4¢ to 15.91¢.</li>
<li>Oklahoma: up 2.8%, from 14.42¢ to 14.82¢.</li>
<li>Louisiana: up 2.5%, from 12.55¢ to 12.87¢.</li>
</ul>
<p>Rising prices help solar, because each kWh your panels make is worth more. Our example assumes prices rise 2.5% a year. A one-year jump is not a trend, so do not bank on it. Our guide on <a href="/guides/solar-payback-if-electricity-prices-rise/">solar payback if electricity prices rise</a> shows how sensitive the result is.</p>
<h2 id="what-a-typical-bill-looks-like">What a typical bill looks like</h2>
<p>If a household uses 900 kWh in a month, the statewide average price works out to roughly these bills. This is an assumption, not a forecast. EIA averages include taxes and fees, and your bill depends on your rate plan.</p>
<ul>
<li>Texas: about $143 a month.</li>
<li>Oklahoma: about $133 a month.</li>
<li>Arkansas: about $129 a month.</li>
<li>Louisiana: about $116 a month.</li>
</ul>
<p>These bills are modest next to many other states. See <a href="/electricity-prices/">electricity prices by state</a> to compare. If your bill is already low, solar has less to offset, and cheaper steps may deliver more. Our guide on <a href="/guides/how-to-lower-your-electric-bill/">how to lower your electric bill</a> covers them.</p>
<h2 id="questions-to-ask-before-you-sign">Questions to ask before you sign</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>How does my utility credit exports?</strong> Ask for the rate in writing. Full retail, a lower &quot;avoided cost&quot; rate and a time-based rate give very different results.</li>
<li><strong>Do I use power while the sun is up?</strong> Our example assumes 40% of use happens in daylight. A home that is empty all day exports more, so credit rules matter more.</li>
<li><strong>What is the all-in price per watt?</strong> Our example uses $3500 per kW. Berkeley Lab reports a median of $4.0 per watt for host-owned systems installed in 2024, with a range of $3.0 to $5.2 per watt, before incentives. Get several quotes.</li>
<li><strong>Are there state or local incentives?</strong> The federal Section 25D credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. DSIRE keeps a database of current state and utility programs.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>
<p>Solar can work in Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas and Louisiana, but it is a slower and more policy-dependent investment than in high-price states. The best case in our sample is about 14 years. The weaker cases do not pay back inside 25 years. Do not rely on a regional average. Use your own rate and your own quote in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>, and compare with the <a href="/solar-panels/">state page for solar panels</a> where you live.</p>
