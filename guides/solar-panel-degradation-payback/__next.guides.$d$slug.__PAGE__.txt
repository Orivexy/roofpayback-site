22:Tffe,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">The short answer</p>
<p>Solar panels lose a small share of their output every year, a process called degradation. The best-known figure comes from a 2012 review by researchers at the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (now the National Laboratory of the Rockies), which assembled &quot;nearly 2000 degradation rates, measured on individual modules or entire systems,&quot; and found &quot;a median value of 0.5%/year.&quot; Our model uses that median.</p>
<p>At 0.5% a year, a system still makes 88.7% of its first-year output in year 25. To show what a worse-than-median panel would do, we also ran 0.8% a year, which leaves 82.5% in year 25. In six states, the faster rate delayed payback by 0 to 0.4 years. It mattered more for the 25-year total than for the payback date.</p>
</div><h2 id="what-degradation-looks-like-in-kwh">What degradation looks like in kWh</h2>
<p>Take the example home in Houston: a 7 kW south-facing system that makes about 9,950 kWh in its first year. At 0.5% a year it makes about 8,820 kWh in year 25; at 0.8%, about 8,200. Over 25 years the slower rate delivers the equivalent of 23.6 full first years of output, the faster rate 22.7, a difference of about 4% of lifetime production.</p>
<p>The model applies one steady rate every year. Real panels can lose more in the first year and less afterward, and some models carry a lower or higher warranted rate. And a median means half of the rates in the review were higher than 0.5%.</p>

<p>Three things stand out.</p>
<p><strong>Payback barely moves.</strong> It lands in the first half of the system&#39;s life, when the panels have hardly faded. Most of the lost output comes later.</p>
<p><strong>Arizona hardly notices.</strong> In Phoenix about 6 kW covers this home&#39;s use, so the 7 kW system makes more than the credit cap allows and part of its surplus already earns nothing. Losing some of that surplus to aging costs only $268 over 25 years.</p>
<p><strong>A low export credit makes the rate cost less in dollars but leaves less margin.</strong> In Texas, the faster rate trims the 25-year gain by $1,941 with full credit and by $485 at quarter credit, because much of the lost output would have been exported at a low value. But at quarter credit the gain was thin to begin with. Louisiana is the case where this applies to new systems: its Public Service Commission credits exports from systems installed after 2019 at avoided cost, and at quarter credit the example does not pay back within 25 years at either rate.</p>

33:T85a,<h2 id="when-degradation-deserves-more-attention">When degradation deserves more attention</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>High installed cost.</strong> A late payback means the faded years carry more of the burden. <a href="/guides/solar-panel-cost-by-state/">Solar panel cost by state</a> shows how much the price per watt moves the result.</li>
<li><strong>Cheap power.</strong> Each lost kWh is worth little, but so is every kWh, and the margin over 25 years is thin; see <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-where-electricity-is-cheap/">solar where electricity is cheap</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Long financing.</strong> If loan payments run past year 15, the system&#39;s later output pays for them. <a href="/guides/solar-loan-vs-cash/">Solar loan vs. cash</a> covers the trade-off.</li>
</ul>
<p>What the model leaves out can matter more than the rate: inverter replacement during 25 years, shade from growing trees, and snow or soiling. The <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> page lists what is included.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-check-in-a-quote">What to check in a quote</h2>
<ol>
<li>The warranted yearly degradation rate and the guaranteed output at year 25, for the exact panel model.</li>
<li>Whether the installer&#39;s production estimate assumes degradation at all, and at what rate.</li>
<li>Who pays for an inverter replacement, and when its warranty ends.</li>
</ol>
<p>Then enter the warranted rate in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> and compare it with 0.5%. If the payback only works at the slower rate, the project has little room for anything else to go wrong; the <a href="/guides/solar-quote-checklist/">quote checklist</a> covers the other terms to compare.</p>
<h2 id="where-degradation-ranks">Where degradation ranks</h2>
<p>Among the inputs that set payback, degradation is one of the smallest. Your price per kWh, how exports are credited and your installed price each move the result by years; going from 0.5% to 0.8% moved it by 0.4 years at most in our six states. Treat the warranty figure as a check on panel quality and a modest adjustment to lifetime savings, not as the deciding number.</p>
