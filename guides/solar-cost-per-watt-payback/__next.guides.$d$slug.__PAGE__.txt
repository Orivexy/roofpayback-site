23:T8d7,<div class="short-answer"><h2 id="short-answer">Short answer</h2>
<p>The price per watt on your solar quote changes your payback time a lot, but how much depends on your state. In our example home, a drop from $3500 per kW to $2500 per kW cuts payback by about four years in Texas, and by less than two years in California. A cheaper quote matters most where electricity savings are smaller each year. This guide shows the numbers and explains why.</p>
</div><h2 id="what-cost-per-watt-means">What &quot;cost per watt&quot; means</h2>
<p>Installers often quote a system by its price per watt. It is the total installed cost divided by the system size. A 7 kW system at $3500 per kW costs $24500 in total. At $2500 per kW, the same system costs $17500.</p>
<p>A kW (kilowatt) is a thousand watts. A kWh (kilowatt-hour) is the energy a 1 kW device uses in one hour. Your bill is based on kWh, and your solar system is paid back by the kWh it saves you.</p>
<p>For context, Berkeley Lab reports a median installed price of $4.0 per watt (20th to 80th percentile $3.0 to $5.2 per watt, 2024 dollars, before incentives) for host-owned residential solar systems installed in 2024. Cash-purchased systems had a median of $3.5 per watt and loan-financed systems $4.7 per watt. Your own quotes may differ, so treat these as a range, not a prediction.</p>
<h2 id="the-example-home-and-its-assumptions">The example home and its assumptions</h2>
<p>All numbers below use the same example home. These are the key assumptions:</p>
<ul>
<li>A 7 kW system and 10500 kWh per year of use.</li>
<li>40% of use happens while the sun is up.</li>
<li>No incentives, and $100 per year for maintenance.</li>
<li>Electricity prices rise 2.5% per year, and panels lose 0.5% of output per year.</li>
<li>A 25-year horizon.</li>
<li>Export credits are capped at a year of grid purchases (an annual true-up).</li>
</ul>
<p>Production comes from PVWatts v8, a free solar estimator from the U.S. Department of Energy&#39;s National Laboratory of the Rockies (formerly NREL). We assume south-facing panels, a 20° pitch and 14% losses, with one reference city per state. Prices are the EIA August 2026 average residential prices. See our <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> for details.</p>

<h2 id="why-the-same-price-drop-helps-some-states-more">Why the same price drop helps some states more</h2>
<p>Look at California. Moving from $3500 to $2500 per kW shortens payback from 7.4 years to 5.5 years. In Texas the same move shortens it from 14.3 years to 10.6 years.</p>
<p>The reason is simple. Payback is the system cost divided by yearly savings. When yearly savings are large, the system pays itself back quickly, so there is less time for a price gap to grow. When yearly savings are smaller, every extra dollar of upfront cost takes longer to earn back.</p>
<p>So a lower price per watt is worth the most where each solar kWh saves the least. In those places, a high quote can push payback toward the long end of the panel&#39;s life. To see why prices differ by state, read <a href="/guides/why-electricity-prices-differ-by-state/">why electricity prices differ by state</a> and check the <a href="/electricity-prices/">electricity prices page</a>.</p>
<h2 id="when-a-cheaper-quote-really-matters">When a cheaper quote really matters</h2>
<p>A lower price per watt matters most in these cases:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your state has moderate electricity prices.</strong> Texas, Florida and Arizona in our example show payback moving by several years between the lowest and highest price.</li>
<li><strong>There are no incentives.</strong> The 30% federal Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. With no credit, the sticker price carries more weight. Our guide on <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-without-tax-credit/">whether solar is worth it without the tax credit</a> goes deeper.</li>
<li><strong>You plan to stay a long time.</strong> A long horizon rewards a lower cost, but only if you stay in the home.</li>
</ul>
<p>It matters less in high-price states. In New York, going from $3500 to $2500 per kW moves payback from 9 years to 6.6 years. That is still useful, but both prices pay back well within the 25-year horizon.</p>
<h2 id="when-the-cheapest-quote-is-not-the-best-one">When the cheapest quote is not the best one</h2>
<p>Price per watt is only one number. Before you pick the lowest, compare these things:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Equipment and warranty.</strong> Ask what panels and inverter are included and how long the warranties last.</li>
<li><strong>System size.</strong> A cheaper price per watt on a system that does not fit your use can leave you with less savings. Our guide on <a href="/guides/how-many-solar-panels-do-i-need/">how many solar panels you need</a> can help.</li>
<li><strong>Roof and layout.</strong> Pitch and direction change output. See <a href="/guides/solar-panel-roof-pitch/">solar panel roof pitch</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Financing.</strong> Loan-financed systems often cost more than cash purchases. Read <a href="/guides/solar-loan-vs-cash/">solar loan vs. cash</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Utility rules.</strong> Net metering and solar incentive programs vary by state and utility. DSIRE maintains a database of current programs at <a href="https://programs.dsireusa.org/">programs.dsireusa.org</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Ask each installer for the full, itemized quote. Then divide the total by the system size in watts, so you compare the same thing across bids.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-test-your-own-quote">How to test your own quote</h2>
<p>Use this short routine:</p>
<ol>
<li>Find the total price and the system size in kW.</li>
<li>Divide price by size to get your cost per kW.</li>
<li>Compare it to the table above and to the Berkeley Lab range.</li>
<li>Run your numbers in our <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>, using your own electricity rate.</li>
<li>Compare your usage with the <a href="/electricity-cost-calculator/">electricity cost calculator</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>For more on how payback is figured, see <a href="/guides/solar-payback-period-explained/">solar payback period explained</a> and <a href="/guides/best-states-for-solar-payback/">best states for solar payback</a>. State pages such as <a href="/solar-panels/texas/">solar panels in Texas</a> show local assumptions.</p>
<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>
<p>A lower cost per watt shortens payback in every state we modeled. The gain is biggest where electricity savings are smaller, such as Texas, Florida and Arizona in our example, and smaller where savings are already large, such as California. Do not choose on price alone. Compare equipment, warranty, system size and financing, then test the quote with your own rate and usage. Your actual payback depends on your utility, your roof and your final contract.</p>
