23:Ta68,<div class="short-answer"><h2 id="short-answer">Short answer</h2>
<p>Solar panels in New England face a trade-off. The region has some of the highest electricity prices in the country, which speeds up payback. It also has less yearly sun than the Southwest, and winter output is low. In our example home, the high prices win. Maine, Massachusetts and New Hampshire show some of the shorter paybacks outside Hawaii and California.</p>
<p>Your real result depends on your utility, your roof and your quote. Treat the numbers below as a starting point, not a promise.</p>
</div><h2 id="the-example-home">The example home</h2>
<p>All figures use our standard example home: a 7 kW system costing $24,500 with no incentives, in a home using 10,500 kWh a year. Every other assumption is listed in the <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a>.</p>
<p>Production comes from PVWatts v8 for a south-facing roof at a 20° pitch with 14% losses, using one reference city per state. PVWatts is a free solar production estimator from the U.S. Department of Energy&#39;s National Laboratory of the Rockies. Prices are EIA August 2026 residential averages. See our <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> for the full math.</p>

<p>Vermont&#39;s summer is about twice its winter. January in Vermont is just 65 kWh per kW, against 137 kWh in July. Snow on panels and short days are part of the reason. These monthly figures come from the same model, so real snow cover could lower winter output further.</p>
<p>Because many utilities settle credits on an annual basis, a strong summer can offset a weak winter on paper. How that works depends on your utility. Read <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">net metering vs. net billing</a> and <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-winter-by-state/">solar panels in winter by state</a> for more.</p>

<p>Even at a quarter credit, all six states pay back within 25 years in this model. Compare that with Texas, where our <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-in-texas/">Texas guide</a> shows longer paybacks at lower prices. <p>The order follows price more than sun. Maine has the highest price of the six, so it pays back fastest despite its modest output. Vermont has both the lowest output and a lower price, so it is slowest.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-change-your-result">What can change your result</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Incentives.</strong> Our example uses none. The 30% federal Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, per the IRS. State and utility programs vary, and DSIRE keeps a database of current programs. See <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-without-tax-credit/">is solar worth it without the tax credit</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Installed price.</strong> Berkeley Lab reports a median installed price of $4.0 per watt for host-owned systems installed in 2024, with a range of $3.0 to $5.2 per watt before incentives. Our example uses $3500 per kW. A higher quote lengthens payback. Learn more in <a href="/guides/solar-cost-per-watt-payback/">solar cost per watt and payback</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Your usage.</strong> The example home uses 10500 kWh per year. A smaller home saves less.</li>
<li><strong>Roof shape and shade.</strong> Tilt, direction and trees all matter. See <a href="/guides/solar-panel-roof-pitch/">roof pitch</a> and <a href="/guides/east-west-facing-solar-panels/">east-west panels</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Future prices.</strong> We assume 2.5% yearly increases. If prices fall, as in Connecticut over the past year, savings shrink. See <a href="/guides/solar-payback-if-electricity-prices-rise/">payback if electricity prices rise</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="steps-to-take-next">Steps to take next</h2>
<ol>
<li>Pull 12 months of bills and find your yearly kWh use.</li>
<li>Find your utility&#39;s export credit rules.</li>
<li>Run your numbers in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>.</li>
<li>Get several written quotes and compare the price per watt.</li>
<li>Look at your state page, such as <a href="/solar-panels/new-hampshire/">New Hampshire solar panels</a> or <a href="/solar-panels/maine/">Maine solar panels</a>.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>
<p>New England has less sun and a weak winter, but its high electricity prices carry the math. In our example home, paybacks at full credit run from 8 years in Maine to 11.3 years in Vermont. Credit rules, your quote and your roof can move that either way. Use the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> with your own bill before you decide.</p>
