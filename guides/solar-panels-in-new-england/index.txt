33:T1188,<div class="short-answer"><p class="short-answer-label">The verdict</p>
<p>New England has some of the shortest solar paybacks outside Hawaii and California because it has some of the highest electricity prices. The six states of the Census Bureau&#39;s New England division make similar amounts of power per kW, so the gaps between them come almost entirely from price. In our example, full-credit payback runs from 8.2 years in Maine to 10.9 in Vermont.</p>
<p>Two things are specific to the region. Typical homes use much less electricity than our example home, so right-sized systems are small. And the rules for exported power differ more from state to state than the sunshine does: Connecticut replaced net metering in 2022, Maine uses kWh credits that expire after a year, and Vermont adjusts its net-metering credits every two years.</p>

<p>Output varies by less than 8% across the region; price varies by more than 25%. Even at quarter credit, every state pays back within 17 years.</p>
<p>Connecticut is the one state whose price fell over the year. A single year&#39;s move is not a trend, and over a longer span all six rose: comparing the 12 months to September 2017 with the 12 months to September 2026 in EIA&#39;s utility data, prices grew about 7.2% a year in Maine, 5.6% in Rhode Island, 4.9% in Massachusetts, 4% in New Hampshire and 3.5% in Connecticut and Vermont, before inflation.</p>

42:Tc53,<h2 id="how-each-state-credits-exports">How each state credits exports</h2>
<p>This is where New England differs most, and where the half- and quarter-credit columns become relevant.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Connecticut.</strong> According to the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority, the Residential Renewable Energy Solutions program, &quot;launched in January 2022,&quot; replaces the legacy net metering program. It has a Buy-All tariff and a Netting tariff, with rates updated for projects that enroll in 2026. The full-credit column is not a given for a new system; ask which tariff and rate the installer used.</li>
<li><strong>Maine.</strong> The Public Utilities Commission&#39;s Net Energy Billing kWh Credit Program is open to all utility customers and gives kWh credits on the bill for power sent to the grid. Unused credits expire after 12 months, which works much like the yearly settlement in our model.</li>
<li><strong>Vermont.</strong> The Public Utility Commission reviews and approves each utility&#39;s net-metering tariff and updates net-metering rate adjustors every two years; a 2026 biennial update is listed on its site. Check the credit value that applies on the date you register.</li>
<li><strong>Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Rhode Island.</strong> We could not confirm the current residential rules from the regulators&#39; own pages for this update. Ask your utility how an exported kWh is credited, then check its tariff with the Massachusetts Department of Public Utilities, the New Hampshire Public Utilities Commission or the Rhode Island Public Utilities Commission.</li>
</ul>
<p><a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">Net metering vs. net billing</a> explains how these arrangements change payback.</p>
<h2 id="winter-in-new-england">Winter in New England</h2>
<p>Winter output is low but not extreme by national standards: the summer-to-winter output ratio is 1.6 to 1.7 in five of the six states and 2 in Vermont. In our model, settling credits monthly instead of yearly added only 0.1 years in Vermont and 0.2 in Maine, because the example system&#39;s summer surplus is modest there. Snow on the panels is not included. <a href="/guides/solar-panels-in-winter-by-state/">Solar panels in winter by state</a> has the full comparison.</p>
<h2 id="before-you-get-quotes">Before you get quotes</h2>
<ol>
<li>Total 12 months of kWh from your bills; expect a smaller system than the example.</li>
<li>Find out which credit arrangement your utility offers new systems, and its current rate.</li>
<li>Compare quotes on price per watt against the $3.50 used here; <a href="/guides/solar-panel-cost-by-state/">solar panel cost by state</a> shows how much that moves payback.</li>
<li>Run your numbers in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>, and see the <a href="/solar-panels/maine/">Maine</a>, <a href="/solar-panels/connecticut/">Connecticut</a> or <a href="/solar-panels/massachusetts/">Massachusetts</a> pages for local detail.</li>
</ol>
