24:T99d,<div class="short-answer"><h2 id="in-one-minute">In one minute</h2>
<p>Waiting only makes sense if the price drop you expect is big enough to beat the savings you give up while you wait, and nobody can promise that drop. In the example home in Texas, a system at <strong>$2,500/kW</strong> pays back in 10.6 years, against 14.3 years at <strong>$3,500/kW</strong>. In California the same two prices give 5.5 and 7.4 years. Use the steps below to test your own quote against those numbers.</p>
</div><h2 id="step-1-write-down-what-waiting-actually-costs">Step 1: Write down what waiting actually costs</h2>
<p>Start with a number you can check: what the system would save in its first year. For the example home (7 kW, 10,500 kWh a year, $24,500 installed, no incentives; see the <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a>), the first-year savings with full export credit are <strong>$3,124</strong> in Los Angeles and <strong>$2,590</strong> in New York City.</p>
<p>In Houston they are $1,583, in Phoenix $1,580 and in Orlando $1,574. Every year you wait is one of those years you never get back, so the lower the number, the cheaper waiting is.</p>
<p>kWh means kilowatt-hour, the unit your utility bills you in. If you need a refresher, read <a href="/guides/what-is-a-kilowatt-hour/">what a kilowatt-hour is</a>.</p>

35:T1647,<h2 id="step-3-check-what-your-quote-really-costs-per-watt">Step 3: Check what your quote really costs per watt</h2>
<p>Berkeley Lab reports a median installed price of $4.0 per watt for host-owned residential systems installed in 2024, with a 20th to 80th percentile range of $3.0 to $5.2 per watt (2024 dollars, before incentives). Cash-purchased systems had a median of $3.5 per watt and loan-financed systems $4.7 per watt.</p>
<p>Divide your quote by the system size in watts. If you land near the loan-financed median, part of that gap may be financing cost rather than hardware, which is worth asking about. <a href="/guides/solar-loan-vs-cash/">Solar loan vs. cash</a> explains why those two medians differ.</p>
<p>A quote well above the range is a reason to collect more bids today. It is not, on its own, a reason to wait years. Our <a href="/guides/solar-quote-checklist/">solar quote checklist</a> lists what to compare.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-run-the-waiting-math-both-ways">Step 4: Run the waiting math both ways</h2>
<p>Waiting is a trade: you might pay less later, but you give up savings now. Take Texas at the example price of $3,500/kW. The first year alone would save $1,583. Add a few years and that is real money you left on the table.</p>
<p>The price drop has to cover that, and no one can say whether it will arrive. Prices can move either way. This guide makes no forecast; it only shows how big a drop would need to be for your state.</p>
<p>Try your own numbers in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> at the current price and at a lower one. If the lower price shortens payback by only a couple of years but you would wait several, waiting loses on paper.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-account-for-electricity-prices-which-move-too">Step 5: Account for electricity prices, which move too</h2>
<p>Panels are not the only price in the equation. What your utility charges per kWh decides how fast the panels earn their cost back. The U.S. average residential price was 18.16 cents per kWh in August 2026, up from 17.57 cents a year earlier.</p>

<h2 id="step-6-remember-the-tax-credit-is-already-gone">Step 6: Remember the tax credit is already gone</h2>
<p>The IRS says the 30% federal Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. So there is no deadline to chase for it, and waiting will not bring it back.</p>
<p>State and utility programs do vary, and <a href="https://programs.dsireusa.org/">DSIRE</a> keeps a database of them. Check your own utility before you decide; our guide on <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-without-tax-credit/">solar without the tax credit</a> shows the numbers with no incentives at all.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-decide-using-a-simple-rule">Step 7: Decide using a simple rule</h2>
<p>Set your two scenarios side by side: buy at today&#39;s quote, or wait for a specific lower quote. Pick the lower price per kW you would honestly need, find its payback in Step 2, and compare it with the years you would spend waiting.</p>
<p>If your state&#39;s first-year savings are high, as in California and New York, waiting is expensive. If they are modest, as in Houston, Phoenix or Orlando, the stakes of waiting are lower, but so is the reward of acting. Check your state page, such as <a href="/solar-panels/texas/">Texas</a> or <a href="/solar-panels/california/">California</a>, for local inputs.</p>
<h2 id="mistakes-that-cost-money">Mistakes that cost money</h2>
<p>Waiting for a price you have no reason to expect is the costliest one. A second is judging only the sticker price while ignoring that a cheap system on a shaded or poorly angled roof makes less power. Check <a href="/guides/solar-panel-roof-pitch/">roof pitch</a> and shade first.</p>
<p>A third is treating one quote as the market. Compare at least a few, and compare per watt. A fourth is assuming a lower price changes the answer in low-price electricity states by much: where power is cheap, payback stays long at every price, as <a href="/guides/is-solar-worth-it-where-electricity-is-cheap/">this guide on cheap-electricity states</a> shows.</p>
<h2 id="your-checklist">Your checklist</h2>
<ul>
<li>Look up your state&#39;s payback at $3,500/kW, $3,000/kW and $2,500/kW in the table above.</li>
<li>Divide your quote by system watts and compare it with the $3.0 to $5.2 per watt Berkeley Lab range.</li>
<li>Note your first-year savings; that is the cost of waiting one year.</li>
<li>Run your own address in the <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a>.</li>
<li>Check your utility&#39;s current <a href="/electricity-prices/">electricity price</a> and any state program on DSIRE.</li>
<li>Collect several quotes before deciding, and set a specific target price if you do choose to wait.</li>
</ul>
