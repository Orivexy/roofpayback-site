67:T1e28,<div class="short-answer"><h2 id="short-answer">Short answer</h2>
<p>Charging an electric car at home costs whatever your home electricity costs. So the state you live in matters a lot. In our example of 3500 kWh of home charging per year, the yearly cost runs from about $445 in Louisiana to about $1680 in Hawaii.</p>
<p>These are estimates, not quotes. They use each state&#39;s average home electricity price from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) for July 2026. Your own cost depends on your utility, your rate plan, and how much you drive.</p>
</div><h2 id="how-we-estimated-the-cost">How we estimated the cost</h2>
<p>A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit your utility uses to bill you for electricity. EV efficiency is often measured in kWh per 100 miles. The Department of Energy&#39;s Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC) explains the basic math with an example: &quot;If electricity costs ¢10.7 per kWh and the vehicle consumes 27 kWh to travel 100 miles, the cost per mile is about $0.03.&quot;</p>
<p>We used the same idea for every state, with these assumptions:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>3500 kWh per year</strong> of home charging. This is an example amount, not a national average. Drive more and you will use more.</li>
<li><strong>All of it is charged at home</strong>, billed at the state&#39;s <strong>average residential price</strong> for July 2026 from the EIA.</li>
<li><strong>No special EV rates.</strong> EIA&#39;s average is utility revenue (including taxes and fees) divided by kWh sold. The per-kWh rate on your bill, or an EV time-of-use rate, may be higher or lower.</li>
</ul>
<p>The EIA publishes these prices every month in its Electric Power Monthly. Recent months are preliminary, so they can be revised. See our <a href="/electricity-prices/">electricity prices by state</a> page for the latest numbers and our <a href="/methodology/">methodology</a> for how we use them.</p>

78:T118c,<h2 id="cheapest-and-most-expensive-states">Cheapest and most expensive states</h2>
<p>The gap between states is large. It comes almost entirely from the price per kWh.</p>
<p><strong>Lowest example costs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Louisiana: 12.72¢ per kWh, about $445 per year</li>
<li>Nevada: 12.77¢ per kWh, about $447 per year</li>
<li>Utah: 13.12¢ per kWh, about $459 per year</li>
<li>North Dakota: 13.41¢ per kWh, about $469 per year</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Highest example costs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Hawaii: 48¢ per kWh, about $1680 per year</li>
<li>California: 33.61¢ per kWh, about $1176 per year</li>
<li>Maine: 32.41¢ per kWh, about $1134 per year</li>
<li>Massachusetts: 30.49¢ per kWh, about $1067 per year</li>
</ul>
<p>The Northeast, California, Alaska and Hawaii sit near the top. Many states in the South, the Mountain West and the Plains sit near the bottom.</p>
<p>Prices also move. Hawaii&#39;s average was 39.36¢ a year earlier, a change of 22%. Maine went from 27.98¢ to 32.41¢. A few states fell, such as Connecticut, which went from 27.6¢ to 24.16¢. So the same driving can cost more or less from one year to the next.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-change-your-real-cost">What can change your real cost</h2>
<p>The table is a starting point. Several things can push your cost up or down:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your rate plan.</strong> The AFDC notes that &quot;Some utilities offer residential time-of-use rates or other rate incentives for charging infrastructure owners.&quot; A time-of-use rate charges different prices at different hours. If your utility has one, charging at off-peak hours may cost less than the state average. Check with your utility.</li>
<li><strong>How much you drive.</strong> If you charge less than 3500 kWh a year, your cost will be lower. If you charge more, it will be higher.</li>
<li><strong>Public charging.</strong> The AFDC says &quot;Most EV owners do the majority of their charging at home&quot;. Charging away from home is priced differently and is not included here.</li>
<li><strong>Your equipment.</strong> Many drivers use the basic Level 1 cord that comes with the car. The AFDC says many owners can meet daily needs this way, &quot;requiring no additional cost or installation, provided that a power outlet on a dedicated branch circuit is available near their parking location.&quot; A faster Level 2 charger may need an electrician, and local permits may be required.</li>
<li><strong>Fixed fees.</strong> Monthly customer charges are on your bill no matter how much you charge, so charging an EV does not raise them. Because the EIA average spreads those charges over every kWh, the per-kWh rate on your own bill is the better guide to the extra cost.</li>
</ul>
<p>To get a better number, look at your own bill. Find your price per kWh (or your time-of-use prices) and multiply it by the kWh you expect to charge.</p>
<h2 id="can-rooftop-solar-lower-ev-charging-costs">Can rooftop solar lower EV charging costs?</h2>
<p>It can, but it depends on your home and your utility. Solar panels make power while the sun is up, while many people charge their cars overnight. How much a solar system helps then depends on how your utility credits the power you send to the grid. Our guide on <a href="/guides/net-metering-vs-net-billing-payback/">net metering vs. net billing</a> explains the difference.</p>
<p>Also note that the 30% federal Residential Clean Energy Credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Net metering and solar incentive programs vary by state and utility. The DSIRE database lists current programs.</p>
<p>If you add an EV, your yearly use goes up, which can change how big a system makes sense. You can test your own numbers in our <a href="/solar-calculator/">solar calculator</a> or read <a href="/guides/how-many-solar-panels-do-i-need/">how many solar panels you need</a>. For state details, see our <a href="/solar-panels/">solar panels by state</a> pages.</p>
<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>
<p>Charging an EV at home is cheapest where electricity is cheapest. In our example of 3500 kWh a year at July 2026 average prices, that is about $445 in Louisiana and about $1680 in Hawaii, with most states in between. Your real cost depends on your utility, your rate plan and your driving. Check your bill&#39;s price per kWh, ask your utility about time-of-use rates, and use our <a href="/electricity-prices/">electricity prices</a> pages to compare.</p>
