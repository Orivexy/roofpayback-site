Home energy calculators

Three tools for the three questions people bring to RoofPayback: what power costs you, whether solar pays back, and whether a battery is worth adding. They start from official data (the U.S. average home price was 18.17¢ per kWh in September 2026) and let you replace every assumption with the numbers on your bill and your quote.

  • Solar payback calculator

    How many years until rooftop solar pays for itself, the savings in year one and over 25 years, and the system size that fits your use.

    You need: Your state or city, yearly kWh (or a monthly bill), and the installed price from a quote.

    Open the calculator

  • Electricity cost calculator

    What a number of kWh costs in your state, and your own monthly bill from your plan's energy price, time-of-use prices and fixed charges.

    You need: Your monthly kWh; for your own bill, the per-kWh price and the fixed charge printed on it.

    Open the calculator

  • Home battery payback calculator

    Whether storing solar or off-peak power pays for a battery before it wears out, and the price gap it needs to break even.

    You need: Your peak price, the export credit or off-peak price you would otherwise get, and a battery quote.

    Open the calculator

Which one to start with

  • Your bill looks high: start with the electricity cost calculator to get your all-in price per kWh, then compare it with your state's average.
  • You have a solar quote: put its price and size into the solar payback calculator, using the all-in price from your bill, and check the export credit your utility pays.
  • Your utility pays little for exported solar or has time-of-use rates: test a battery in the battery payback calculator.

Every calculation runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent to us. How the numbers are produced is set out in the methodology. Results are estimates, not quotes, and not financial, tax or legal advice.
