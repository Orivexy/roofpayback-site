Editorial policy

Last updated: 2026-11-18

RoofPayback explains what U.S. households pay for electricity and whether rooftop solar pays back where they live. This page describes, without gloss, how each article is produced and what we do and don't check.

Who is responsible

Eric Muñiz Genebriera runs RoofPayback. He wrote the payback model and the data pipeline, sets the editorial rules on this page and handles corrections. There is no newsroom and no sponsored content: every page is published under the RoofPayback name, and he is accountable for it.

Where the facts come from

  • Electricity prices: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Electric Power Monthly (state averages by sector, monthly).
  • Solar output: PVWatts, the Department of Energy's model run by the National Laboratory of the Rockies, queried for each state and city we cover.
  • Payback figures: our own model, published in full on the methodology page, applied to the same example home everywhere so results can be compared.
  • Installed costs and incentives: Berkeley Lab's Tracking the Sun, the IRS, and the DSIRE database of state and utility programs.

We don't use installer marketing, survey "estimates" without a method, or figures we can't trace to one of these sources.

How an article is written

  1. Our code builds a fact pack for the topic from the sources above. Every number an article may use is in that pack.
  2. An AI model drafts the article from the fact pack, in one of several fixed formats (data report, Q&A, step-by-step guide, comparison, explainer and others).
  3. Automated checks then reject the draft if any figure is not in the fact pack or in a quote re-fetched from an official .gov page, if a source is not on our allow-list, if an internal link is broken, if it repeats an earlier article, or if it contains promotional claims such as guaranteed savings.
  4. Drafts that fail stay unpublished. Articles that pass carry the line "Figures checked against EIA and PVWatts data", which links to this page: the check is automated, and we don't claim an expert review that didn't take place.
  5. Articles are re-read periodically and rewritten when they overlap with another article, when data changes or when a reader reports a problem.

State, city and price pages are not written by AI: they are generated directly from the data, so their numbers update when the source data does.

What we won't do

  • Invent people, quotes, testimonials, reader questions or case studies. Households in examples are hypothetical and say so.
  • Promise savings. Payback figures are estimates for an example home, and we show how they change with the export credit, the price you pay and your quote.
  • Give tax, legal or investment advice, or rank installers.
  • Let advertisers or affiliate partners review or change content (see how we make money).

Updates and corrections

When EIA publishes a new month, prices on state pages update automatically. Articles state which month their figures come from. Material changes and corrections are listed with their date in the changelog. If you find an error, tell us through the contact page; we reply and fix confirmed errors.
