Common questions

How do I calculate my electricity cost?

Multiply the kWh you use by your price per kWh. For example, 900 kWh at 15¢ per kWh is $135. The EIA state averages used here already include taxes and fees, spread across every kWh sold. Your own bill depends on your utility's rates: fixed monthly charges weigh more if you use little power.

Where do these prices come from?

They are the average residential prices per state from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), September 2026. Recent months are preliminary and may be revised. Your utility's rate can be higher or lower.

How much does 1 kWh of electricity cost?

18.17¢ on average for U.S. homes in September 2026 (EIA), from 13.25¢ to 48.31¢ depending on the state. So 100 kWh costs about $18 and 1,000 kWh about $182 at the national average.

How many kWh does a home use in a month?

The average U.S. residential customer used 885 kWh a month from October 2025 to September 2026 (EIA-861M), but it varies widely by state, climate, heating and cooling; each state's price page shows its own average. Your electric bill shows your exact monthly use; use that number for the most accurate estimate.

How do time-of-use rates change the bill?

On a time-of-use plan each kWh costs more during the peak window (often late afternoon and evening) and less off-peak. The bill is peak kWh × peak price plus off-peak kWh × off-peak price, plus fixed charges. Shifting use such as laundry, dishwashing or EV charging out of the peak window lowers it; the saving per kWh moved is the gap between the two prices. Choose "My bill's rates" above to enter your own plan.

Should I add my fixed monthly charge to the state average?

No. EIA's state average is utility revenue, including fixed charges, taxes and fees, divided by every kWh sold, so fixed charges are already inside it. Add a fixed charge only when you enter your own energy price from your bill.
